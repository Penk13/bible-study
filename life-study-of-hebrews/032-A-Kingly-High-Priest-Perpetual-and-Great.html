Hebrews · Message 32

A Kingly High Priest, Perpetual and Great

The Christ on the throne in heaven is experienced subjectively in our spirit. There He is our kingly High Priest according to Melchisedec, not offering sacrifices for pitiful sinners but ministering the processed God as bread and wine to victorious fighters.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Subjectively Objective: In fact Christ is in heaven; in experience we touch Him in our spirit, where the throne of grace is joined to us.
  • Come Forward: The writer, already within the veil, calls us to come forward to the Holy of Holies, the throne of grace, and God ().
  • Bread and Wine: Melchisedec did not offer for Abraham's sin; he met a returning victor with bread and wine ().
  • Kingly: King of righteousness, King of peace, from the royal tribe of Judah ().
  • Perpetual and Great: Without genealogy, He abides a priest continually, and He is greater than Abraham and all the Levitical priests.

Bethel in Our Spirit

If the throne of grace is only far away in heaven, how can we on earth come forward to it? At Bethel, the house of God, a ladder joined earth and heaven (). Today God's house is being built in spirit (), so Christ as the ladder joins our spirit to the throne. This is why Hebrews speaks of dividing soul and spirit () right before the throne of grace.

⚡

In Fact: Objective

Like electricity in a distant power plant, Christ is on the throne in heaven.

💡

In Experience: Subjective

Through the "wires," the heavenly ladder, He is applied in our spirit day by day.

Aaron Is Past; Melchisedec Is Present

The first priest in the Bible is Melchisedec, not Aaron. Psalm 110 is the bridge from Genesis 14 to Hebrews, passing over Leviticus. While we slaughter the enemies within, the self, the natural mind, the wild emotion, the stubborn will, He intercedes (). When the fight is over, He comes with bread and wine.

Order of Aaron

Sacrifices for Sinners

Offering for sin: true, but past and on the negative side.

Order of Melchisedec

Supply for Victors

Ministering the processed God as grace in righteousness and peace.

Kingly, Perpetual, Great

A priest who only offers for sinners need not be a king. A priest who ministers God to victors must be the King of righteousness and of peace, combining kingship with priesthood for God's building and glory.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Is Christ in heaven or in your spirit?Fact and experience.

Both: Doctrinally He is on the throne in heaven. Experientially, the throne of grace is joined to our spirit by Christ as the heavenly ladder.

Turn Within: When we leave the mind and emotions and turn to the spirit, Bethel is here and heaven's traffic flows.

Do you come to your High Priest as a pitiful sinner or as a fighter?Abraham returning from battle.

Not Begging: Abraham did not plead for his sins; he had just defeated the kings.

Slaughter, Then Feast: Our part is to slay the inward enemies; His part is to intercede and then refresh us with the processed God.

Why must this High Priest also be a King?Righteousness, peace, building.

Order for Building: His kingship keeps a peaceful order through righteousness, the condition God's building needs.

Supply for Building: His priesthood ministers all the supply; together they bring forth God's glory.

What does it mean to you that He abides a priest perpetually?No coming or going.

Constant Presence: He may seem to withdraw, yet He never says good-bye; in the morning He is still there.

Daily Supply: The eternal One is our unfailing source of bread and wine, day after day.
