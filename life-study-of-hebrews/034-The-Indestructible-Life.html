Hebrews · Message 34

The Indestructible Life

Christ's divine priesthood is constituted with the indestructible life. This life saves us to the uttermost, not mainly from sin, but from the by-products of death, into Christ's own perfection.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Three Aspects: The Aaronic priesthood offers for sin, the kingly ministers God as our supply, and the divine saves us to the uttermost.
  • God's First Intention: From the tree of life (), God wanted to be eaten; dealing with sin was added after the fall.
  • By-products of Death: Vanity, corruption, bondage, groaning, and decay remain even after sin is dealt with ().
  • Saved to Perfection: "Uttermost" shares a root with perfection; to be saved so is to be glorified, brought into Christ's perfection.
  • The Life Itself: This life is God's, eternal, uncreated, Christ Himself, tested, resurrected, and indissoluble.

Offering, Ministering, Saving

Many Christians stay with the Aaronic priesthood and its sin offering. But eating and drinking God is the worship that most satisfies Him. As we enjoy the kingly priesthood, we also partake of the divine priesthood, which day by day swallows up our sighing and groaning.

What This Life Is

Unbelievers are alienated from the life of God (); we are joined to it. This is the highest life in the universe: not vegetable, animal, angelic, or merely human, but divine.

God's Life, Eternal and Uncreated

Without beginning or end, beyond time and space, yet always creating ().

Christ Himself

Not only functions but a Person (; ).

Tested and Resurrected

Pilate found no fault in Him (); He was dead and is alive forever (; ).

Saving, Reigning, Indissoluble

The Spirit of life () that saves us (), makes us reign (), and brings incorruption ().

"I Can," Not "I Can't"

Death is stronger than every man, but this life is stronger than death. It toured the region of death and walked out; death could not hold it. In the church, losing our temper or being unable to pray and function are death. Life never says "I can't."

Death

"I Can't"

Groaning, sighing, inability, looking at our weakness.

Life

"Here Am I; Send Me"

Praising, functioning, loving one another to the uttermost.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What does Christ save us to the uttermost from?Not only sin.

By-products of Death: Vanity, corruption, bondage, groaning, and decay, which pervade our homes and lives even after sin is forgiven.

Into Perfection: He brings us into His own perfection, where none of these exist: our glorification.

Why can enjoying God be called the best worship?Bread and wine, not kneeling.

God's Intention: From the tree of life, God's desire was to dispense Himself into man as food.

Satisfying Him: The more we eat and drink of Him, the more His heart is satisfied.

How do the world's entertainments compare with this life?A drug versus a cure.

A Drug: Sports, entertainment, and even education may dull the inner groaning, but it returns afterward.

A Cure: Only the divine priesthood, the presence of life, actually diminishes and swallows up that groaning.

Where do you say "I can't" in the church life?Death or life.

Recognize Death: Inability to praise, pray, or speak is not humility; it is death.

Exercise Life: The life within has passed through death and cannot be held down; respond, "Lord, here I am."
