Hebrews · Message 14

Holy Brothers and Partakers of the Heavenly Calling

Only after two chapters of qualifications can Hebrews 3:1 call us "holy brothers, partakers of a heavenly calling." We are brothers of the Firstborn and partners of God's Anointed, being saturated with God's holy nature, and called out of an earthly religion to everything heavenly.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • A Unique Title: No other New Testament book calls us "holy brothers" (). Not sinlessly perfect, but divinely holy.
  • Partners of the Anointed: God appointed the Heir, then anointed Him; we share that anointing as His partners (). This is part of the full gospel.
  • A Higher Gospel: Not only "you are a sinner going to hell," but "you can become a holy brother of the Firstborn Son." Thoughtful people seeking life's meaning are caught by this.
  • Teaification: Christ, the essence of divine holiness, permeates us as tea permeates water. We partake of His holiness () and must pursue it ().
  • Heavenly, Not Earthly: Judaism had an earthly calling. We are called to a heavenly Christ, gift, worship, Jerusalem, and country, with the Spirit within and divine discipline without.

Who the Holy Brothers Are

Hebrews 1–2 supply everything needed to call us holy brothers: standing, qualifications, reality, life, nature, and source.

Our Majesty

The New Testament is not a book of regulations but of holy brothers. If we saw that we are brothers of the Firstborn and partners of God's Anointed, our tastes would change. Low habits would simply lose their appeal.

Partakers of the Heavenly Calling

Everything in Judaism was earthly. Hebrews contrasts it with the heavenly nature of the New Testament: Christ sits in the heavens (), has entered heaven itself (), passed through the heavens (), and is higher than the heavens ().

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why could the writer only call us "holy brothers" in chapter 3, not chapter 1?Review what chapters 1–2 established about Christ and about us.

Qualifications First: We were sinners, death-victims, and captives. Chapters 1–2 show Christ's propitiation, His release of us from death, our birth as His brothers in resurrection, and our partnership in His anointing ().

Now Holy Brothers: With standing, life, nature, and source all supplied, the title fits (). It describes what we are, not merely what we should try to be.

How would the gospel we preach change if it aimed at producing holy brothers?Think of people asking, "What is the purpose of life?"

From Low to High: "You are a sinner going to hell" is not wrong, but it is low. The full gospel says God calls people to believe in His Firstborn Son and become His brothers and partners in God's eternal plan.

Meeting the Real Question: Many students wonder what life is for. This gospel answers them, and thoughtful people are caught by it.

What does "teaification" teach about how we become holy?Compare Hebrews 12:10 with Hebrews 12:14.

Saturation, Not Adjustment: Water becomes tea only by tea being added until every part is permeated. Christ, God's holy nature, saturates us; this is how we partake of His holiness ().

Still Pursuing: Because we are being saturated, we pursue holiness daily (). Without it our fellowship with the Lord breaks, and we cannot see Him.

Where might our worship or Christian life still be "earthly"?Contrast the earthly and heavenly Jerusalem (Galatians 4:25–26).

Shadows and Forms: Earthly worship clings to copies and shadows (), to law and bondage ().

Crossing Over: Our names are in heaven (), our Christ is there, and we have come to the heavenly Jerusalem (). By the Spirit we can live and worship heavenly while still on earth.
