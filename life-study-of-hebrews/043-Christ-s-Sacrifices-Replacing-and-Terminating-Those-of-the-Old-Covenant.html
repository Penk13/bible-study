Hebrews · Message 43

Christ's Sacrifices Replacing and Terminating Those of the Old Covenant

Hebrews 10:1-18 adds a further conclusion for minds steeped in the sin offering: Christ's one sacrifice has replaced and ended all the Levitical offerings. Sin is now a history, and the inward law of life is what remains.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • A Shadow Only: The law's sacrifices could neither purge the conscience nor perfect the worshipper; they only reminded people of sins year by year ().
  • A Body Prepared: Christ came in the flesh to take away the first so He could establish the second, sanctifying us once for all ().
  • Standing vs. Sitting: The priests stood daily because their work was never done; Christ sat down forever because His is finished.
  • Forgotten Sins: The Spirit testifies that God will remember our sins no more, so there is no longer any offering for sin.

What the Law Could Not Do

The law held only the shadow of the coming good things, which are Christ Himself and His work. However often the same offerings were repeated, they could not remove sins, for animal blood never could (). God's intention was that every sin offering would remind the people of their need and point them to the coming Messiah.

Old Covenant

Standing Daily

The same sacrifices, again and again, which can never take away sins ().

New Covenant

Sat Down Forever

One sacrifice for sins, then seated at God's right hand; His sitting proves the work is complete.

Once for All

God's will was to remove the animal offerings and establish Christ's offering in their place. Through it He has done what no other sacrifice could.

Forgiveness Is a Supplement

Quoting Jeremiah 31 again, the Spirit testifies that God will by no means remember our sins (). We naturally rank forgiveness first in the new covenant, but it comes last, as a word of peace. The main items are the law of life within, God being our God, and the inward ability to know Him.

Forgive Means Forget

God not only forgives; He forgets, as though we never sinned. Since He no longer remembers our sins, we should stop talking about sin and become a church that talks about the law of life.

"No longer an offering for sin" () does not mean that a believer who sins cannot be forgiven. It means the sin offering has been completed and ended, so going back to the altar would be meaningless. Our gaze must turn from Christ on the cross to Christ in the heavens.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why did the old sacrifices remind people of sin rather than remove it?Shadow, not reality.

Shadow: Animal blood could not touch the reality of sin or cleanse the conscience.

Pointer: Each yearly offering was meant to show the people their need and turn their eyes to Christ.

What does Christ's sitting down tell you about your sins?Heb. 10:11-12.

Finished: He sits because nothing more needs to be done for sin; the priests stood because their work was never complete.

At Peace: Our conscience can rest on a finished fact rather than on repeated efforts.

Why is forgiveness only a supplement in the new covenant?Three main items.

The Main Things: The law of life written within, God being our God, and our knowing Him inwardly are eternal.

The Supplement: Forgiveness reassures those still troubled by sins, so they can turn their full attention to life.

How can believers today still "go back to the altar"?Sin-conscious living.

Dwelling on Sin: Constantly reviewing our sins acts as if the offering were unfinished.

Moving On: Sin is history; we should enjoy the heavenly Christ as our life and run the race under the new covenant.
