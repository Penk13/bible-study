Hebrews · Message 6

So Great a Salvation

Hebrews unveils a salvation infinitely higher than merely escaping hell or going to heaven: it is the boundless, all-inclusive person of Christ—both the eternal Son of God and the exalted Son of Man—who fights as our Captain leading us into glory, ministers as our heavenly High Priest, and calls us to cross the river out of old religion into His uttermost salvation without drifting.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Transcending the Low Concept of Salvation: Natural religious thought reduces salvation to "going to heaven to avoid hell" or merely receiving objective forgiveness. In Hebrews, salvation is "so great" () because it is a living, all-inclusive divine-human Person—Christ in all the fullness of the Godhead and all the uplifted human virtues!
  • Great in What Christ Is (God & Man): Before Hebrews 2:3, Christ is unveiled as the Son of God, even God Himself (). After 2:3, He is unveiled as the Son of Man (). Christ did not strip off His humanity in resurrection; Stephen saw Him standing as the Son of Man (), and on the throne today He remains the glorified Man Christ Jesus ().
  • The Fighting Captain (Archēgos) and Ministering High Priest: To escape Egypt took one night, but entering the good land of rest requires a lifelong march under our Captain (; ) who pioneers and battles every inch of the way. When we fight the battle of faith, Christ meets us as Melchizedek (), ministering bread and wine to sustain us!
  • Great in Scope & Extent (Past, Present, and Future): Christ accomplished once-for-all purification of sins (; ), tasted death for everyone (), and destroyed the devil on the cross (). Today He is sanctifying us () and saving to the uttermost (). In the coming age, He will bring many sons into glory () and give us to possess the coming inhabited earth ().
  • The Warning: Give Heed, Do Not Drift, Cross the River: Like a boat drifting helplessly in a subtle undertow, believers who neglect this living supply drift away into religion, dead knowledge, or worldly anxiety (). We must give earnest heed, go "outside the camp" (), enter "within the veil" (), and cross every small river in daily life!

Great in What Christ Is: The God-Man in Glory

Why does the writer of Hebrews describe our salvation as "so great" ()? The primary factor is not the number of benefits we receive, but the sheer stature and identity of the One who constitutes it. This salvation is great because it is constituted with the complete Person of Christ:

Son of God · Before Heb. 2:3

Fullness of Deity

He is God Himself on the throne (). He who has seen the Son has seen the Father (). All the infinite divine nature, eternal life, uncreated light, and universal power are the constituent elements of this salvation.

Son of Man · After Heb. 2:3

Uplifted Humanity

He is a real man crowned with glory and honor (, ). Resurrected and ascended, He remains the glorified Man Christ Jesus (; ), infusing His perfect human virtues into our daily walk.

🎪

The "Vanity Fair" of Mere Doctrine

Mental terms, intellectual Bible study, and debating the Trinity apart from experience. Like a bazaar selling empty trinkets, doctrine without living reality becomes our "Chaldea"—a religious trap holding us back from the good land.

🌿

The Living Reality in Spirit

Experiencing the Father, Son, and Spirit as the one living Christ within us today! In our spirit, all the divine attributes and human virtues of this wonderful God-Man become our immediate, practical salvation.

The Fighting Captain and the Ministering High Priest

Our ongoing salvation is not an instantaneous, overnight relocation into heaven. While coming out of Egypt (regeneration and justification) occurred in a single Passover night, possessing the good land requires following our Captain of salvation () through lifelong spiritual warfare:

The Captain (Archēgos) · Heb. 2:10

The Leader Who Fights the Way

An archēgos is a pioneer, leader, and general who not only opens the path but battles through every obstacle. We in the church are an army () fighting our way under His banner to take the good land and enter into glory!

The High Priest (Melchizedek) · Heb. 7:25

The Minister of Bread & Wine

While Christ leads the battle, He is simultaneously our High Priest according to the order of Melchizedek (). Whenever we fight the battle of faith, He appears with bread and wine to nourish, strengthen, and refresh our inner being.

Great in Scope & Extent: Past, Present, and Eternal

The greatness of this salvation is demonstrated across the three dimensions of time: what Christ has accomplished objectively on the cross, what He is ministering subjectively in our spirit today, and the glorious consummation He will bring us into:

Time Horizon The Mighty Works of Christ Key Scriptures Spiritual Reality for Believers

• Tasted death for everyone; abolished death
• Destroyed the devil through His cross

The Warning: Drifting with the Current vs. Crossing the River

Because this salvation is so immense, the writer delivers a solemn warning in : "Therefore we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away... how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation?"

🌊

1. The Danger of Drifting

A boat drifts not by deliberate rebellion, but by simply being unanchored in a moving current. The currents of worldly ambition, religious tradition, or personal analytical thoughts will imperceptibly carry us away from Christ if we do not pay close attention.

⚠️

2. The Trap of Neglect

Neglecting this salvation does not mean rejecting it or losing eternal life; it means taking Christ for granted, remaining passive, and ignoring His daily transmitting supply. Those who neglect it suffer loss and miss the kingdom reward ().

⚓

3. Crossing the River Daily

A true "Hebrew" is a river crosser! Whenever we face offenses, discouragement, or religious deadness, we must cross the river. Leave the old camp () and anchor our soul within the veil () to enjoy the living Christ.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is the traditional evangelical concept of salvation ("going to heaven / escaping hell") considered too low when measured against the "so great a salvation" unveiled in Hebrews 2:3?Examine the constituent elements of this salvation—the Person of Christ, His divinity, His uplifted humanity, and His divine enterprise.

A Low Concept vs. Cosmic Reality: Escaping hell and receiving legal forgiveness are precious, but they are merely the entry gate. Traditional concepts reduce salvation to an objective ticket to heaven, ignoring the organic purpose of God’s economy.

Salvation Is a Living Person: In Hebrews, salvation is nothing less than the living, all-inclusive Christ Himself. It contains all the fullness of the Godhead () and the perfect, resurrected virtues of the Son of Man (; ). To have this salvation is to possess Christ as our present life and coming glory.

The Corporate Goal: This salvation does not leave us as isolated converts; it constitutes us as the many sons brought into glory () and royal partners (metochoi) sharing His universal kingdom inheritance (; ).

How do we practically experience Christ simultaneously as our fighting Captain (Archēgos) and our ministering High Priest (Melchizedek) in our daily Christian journey?Consider the journey from Egypt into Canaan, the nature of spiritual warfare, and Melchizedek ministering bread and wine after Abraham's battle (Genesis 14).

Following the Pioneer: As the Captain of salvation (), Christ does not merely command from afar; He pioneers the trail, cuts through the wilderness, and leads the army of God through battle. In our church life and personal walk, entering into rest is a daily fight of faith against the adversary's lies.

The Supply of Melchizedek: When Abraham returned from defeating the kings, Melchizedek met him not with legal demands or sacrificial blood, but with bread and wine (). Christ only ministers as Melchizedek to those who fight! When we stand for the Lord's interest, He transmits heavenly life supply to sustain our spirit.

No Defeat or Deficiency: Because He is fighting for us and ministering to us, there is never any excuse to be spiritually impoverished. Every battle fought in spirit concludes with the sweet feast of His bread and wine!

What does it mean to "drift away" (Hebrews 2:1) from so great a salvation, and how can good things like doctrinal knowledge, past achievements, or personal thoughts become our "Chaldea"?Contrast active opposition with passive drifting, and consider Abraham's departure from Ur of the Chaldees.

The Insidious Nature of Drifting: Drifting requires no effort; a boat simply floats with the downstream current when it is not securely anchored. Believers rarely wake up intending to forsake Christ; they drift imperceptibly through preoccupation with daily cares, religious complacency, or subtle worldly currents.

Chaldea as Old Background: Chaldea was Abraham's birthplace—the land of idolatry and old origin that he had to leave by crossing the Euphrates. For Christians, "Chaldea" can be traditional Bible doctrines held only in the head, past spiritual victories from decades ago, or an analytical mind that refuses to move into the living experience of Christ.

The Need to Go On: Hebrews is a book of river crossing! What was living and good twenty years ago (or at the Red Sea) must not become an idol today. We must continually leave our old regions and press forward into the present speaking and operation of Christ.

Witness Lee emphasizes that "we need to cross rivers every day"—even after offending our spouse or facing discouragement. How do we practically apply Christ's uttermost salvation (Hebrews 7:25) and succor (Hebrews 2:18) in such moments?Consider how an unresolved offense acts as a veil, and how faith rejects the enemy's lies to lay hold of Christ's living presence.

Crossing Small Daily Rivers: After an offense with a spouse, family member, or saint, our inward condition falls into an "old region." If we remain in our wounded pride or stubbornness, that uncrossed river becomes a veil separating us from the Holy of Holies. To apologize, turn to our spirit, and touch the blood is to cross the river immediately into the enjoyment of salvation!

Refusing the Enemy's Lies: The enemy constantly whispers lies about our weakness, family problems, or the condition of the church meetings. Instead of agreeing with defeat, faith declares: "Christ has destroyed the devil ()! He has tasted death for me, and He is able to succor me in every temptation ()."

Saved to the Uttermost: Because Christ always lives to make intercession for us (), no situation is hopeless. His ongoing heavenly transmission lifts us above every circumstance into His glorious rest.
