Hebrews · Message 4

Christ as the Son of God — as God Superior to the Angels

The book of Hebrews uses the five treasures of Judaism as a contrasting black background to reveal the absolute supremacy of Christ. Having inherited a more excellent name than the angels, Christ as the Son of God is unveiled as the Creator, the Firstborn, the King, and the eternal One across six glorious stages from eternity to eternity.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • The Wisdom of Divine Comparison: The Hebrew believers were hesitating on the bridge between God-ordained Judaism and Christ, pressured by persecution. Rather than rebuking them, the Spirit uses the five treasures of Judaism (God, angels, Moses, Aaron, and the covenant) as a dark backdrop to display the incomparable brightness of Christ in God's economy.
  • The Expressed God vs. The Hidden God: In Judaism, God was genuine yet hidden and approached indirectly through prophets and rituals. In God's New Testament salvation, God is expressed directly in the Son (). The Father is for His being, and the Son is for His being given to reach us, to be tasted, received, and enjoyed day by day (; ).
  • A More Excellent Name (): Christ has inherited the name "The Son." God never addressed an angel as His Son (; ). Angels are created spirits and flames of fire (), ministering servants dispatched to serve us—the heirs of salvation (). The Son is the Creator and the Object of angelic worship ().
  • Sixfold Superiority Over the Angels: Christ’s superiority is systematically proved across six historical and divine stages: 1) His more excellent Name, 2) In Resurrection (germinating the church as Firstborn), 3) In Ascension (seated at God's right hand), 4) In the Second Coming (worshiped by all angels), 5) In the Kingdom (ruling on God’s eternal throne with a scepter of righteousness), and 6) In Eternity (remaining the unchangeable Creator whose years do not fail).

The Strategy of Hebrews: The Fivefold Divine Comparison

The Hebrew believers held Christ with one hand and their ancestral religion with the other. Judaism was not a pagan idolatry; it was ordained and blessed by God through the Old Testament oracles. But it was only a shadow. To move them off the bridge of indecision, the writer presents five foundational contrasts where Christ utterly transcends the highest boasts of Judaism:

Judaism's Cherished Boast The Old Testament Realm (The Shadow) The Realm in God’s Economy (The Reality)
1. God A true but hidden God, known at a distance in terminology (). God directly expressed in the Son, touched, received, and experienced (; ).
2. Angels Created ministers (winds & fire) who mediated the law (; ). The uncreated Son, the Creator worshiped by angels and seated above them ().
3. Moses The foremost human apostle sent from God to man (). Christ the Apostle worthy of more glory than Moses, the Builder of God’s house ().
4. Aaron & Priesthood Mortal priests serving as heavenly court attorneys (). The great High Priest living forever according to the order of Melchizedek (; ).
5. The Covenant The Old Covenant of letters inscribed outwardly on tablets of stone. The New Covenant of life, with the law of life inscribed into our heart and spirit ().

The Mystery of the Son: God Expressed and Reaching Us

Why did God speak in the Son rather than through prophets? The difference is between knowing God as an abstract doctrine and experiencing Him as living reality. Isaiah 9:6 unlocks this divine mystery:

God in Judaism · The Father

“The Father for His Being”

In His essential Godhead, God is the eternal Father. But if He remained only the hidden, transcendent Father in unapproachable light, no human could ever reach, receive, or enjoy Him. He remained an objective object of distant reverence.

God in Salvation · The Son

“The Son for His Being Given”

“Unto us a Son is given… His name shall be called Everlasting Father” (). The Son is the divine gift ()—God Himself coming out of eternity into time, stepping into humanity, touching us, and giving Himself to be our daily living portion!

The Creator vs. Created Servants: Christ and the Angels

Judaism elevated angels because they were radiant celestial beings who stood near God and delivered the law (). But Hebrews strips away every misconception by unveiling the fundamental distinction of their nature and function:

🌪️

The Angels: Created Spirits & Ministers

• Creatures: God makes His angels spirits and His ministers a flame of fire ().
• Subservient Posture: They stand as servants; not one was ever told to sit at God’s right hand ().
• Commissioned for Believers: They are ministering spirits sent forth to serve those who inherit salvation (). Angels do not command us; they serve our salvation!

👑

The Son: The Eternal Creator & Object of Worship

• Creator & God: Addressed directly by the Father as “O God” and “Lord” (, ).
• Enthroned King: Sits perpetually at the Majesty’s right hand with an everlasting scepter of righteousness ().
• Worshiped by All Angels: When the Firstborn comes into the world, God commands: “Let all the angels of God worship Him!” ().

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why did the Holy Spirit use the pedagogical method of comparison instead of rebuking the Hebrew believers who were hesitating between Judaism and Christ?Consider the psychological situation of believers holding on to both Christ and their God-ordained heritage. How does a "black background" reveal the brilliance of white?

The Believers' Plight: The Hebrew believers were not tangled in filthy heathen idols; their background was established by God’s own oracles, complete with the temple, the law, and the Levitical priesthood. In the face of fierce persecution and ostracization from the high priest, they hesitated on the bridge, attempting to hold Christ in one hand and Judaism in the other.

The Wisdom of Comparison: Severe rebukes rarely change an inward heart convinced of its noble religious heritage. The Holy Spirit utilized divine wisdom by comparing the two economies side by side. By presenting the genuine items of Judaism as a dark backdrop, the unmatched whiteness, reality, and living splendor of Christ stood out in irresistible clarity.

Spiritual Principle: We do not leave religion simply by force of argument; we leave it when our eyes are enlightened to see how infinitely superior Christ is to all shadows, forms, and external religious rituals.

How does Isaiah 9:6 unlock the spiritual reality of God as the Father versus God as the Son in our daily Christian experience?Reflect on the phrase: "Unto us a Son is given... His name shall be called... Everlasting Father." Why is the Father for "His being" while the Son is for "His being given"?

The Hidden God vs. The Reaching God: The Father denotes God in His transcendent, eternal being dwelling in unapproachable light. In Judaism, people had the true God, but He remained objective, distant, and known primarily through laws and doctrinal terms.

The Divine Gift: To reach, touch, and enter into man, God gave Himself as the Son (; ). The Son is the Father expressed, embodied, and given to us as our life and subjective enjoyment. When we touch the Son, we touch the Father; when we possess the Son, we possess God Himself.

Practical Enjoyment: Our Christian life is not a cold adherence to monotheistic theology; it is contacting, tasting, and living by the Son who has reached our spirit and makes God real to our daily experience.

Why does Hebrews 1:6 call Christ the "Firstborn" when He comes again into the world, and what is the profound significance of all angels worshiping Him?Contrast Christ as the "only begotten Son" in John 1:14 with Christ as the "firstborn among many brethren" in Romans 8:29. Who are His companions and brothers?

From Only Begotten to Firstborn: In His incarnation and earthly ministry, Christ was God’s only begotten Son (; )—having divinity unique to Himself without human brothers. But through resurrection, His humanity was begotten into divine sonship (; ), and all believers were regenerated as the many sons of God, making Him the Firstborn among many brethren ().

The Angels' Worship: When God brings the Firstborn again into the inhabited earth at His second coming (), He does not return alone; He comes as the Head of a glorified corporate Body of sons. God commands all angels to fall down and worship Him!

Angel vs. Church: Angels are merely servants; they are not brothers of Christ nor sons of God in life. The Son who is worshiped by angels has brought us into His very sonship, making the church far higher in organic standing than celestial messengers.

According to Hebrews 1:14, angels are "ministering spirits sent forth to minister for those who will inherit salvation." How does this deliver us from religious angel-worship or seeking mystical manifestations?Examine the relationship between the heir of salvation (the believer) and the servant (the angel). Why should our attention remain exclusively on Christ?

The Servants of the Heirs: In ancient royal households, guardians and tutors attended to the prince, yet the prince was the heir while the attendants were servants. Hebrews 1:14 establishes that angels are dispatched by God to care for the circumstances and preservation of the believers who are the heirs of salvation.

The Error of Angel Veneration: Throughout church history and in modern charismatic circles, people have fallen into the trap of obsessing over angel visitations, mystical encounters, or supernatural mediation (). But Hebrews declares that angels are merely created winds and flames () serving under Christ’s administration.

Fixing Our Eyes on Christ: We do not pray to angels, seek angels, or boast in angels. We fix our gaze upon the seated Son of God on the throne! While angels perform their providential service invisibly, our spirit communes directly with the living, indwelling Christ who is our all in all.
