Exodus · Message 30

Israel's Experience at Marah

Baptism brings us into the realm of resurrection, where there is no natural supply. There God leads us to bitter waters, and the tree of the cross makes both our situation and our being sweet, so that the Lord becomes our Healer.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • The Wilderness of Shur: Beyond the sea and the wall, it is a realm of separation and of resurrection, where the baptized walk in newness of life ().
  • Three Days, No Water: Three is the number of resurrection; in that realm there is no natural water to rely on ().
  • Bitter Waters: The cloud itself led them to Marah. The people murmured; Moses cried to the Lord ().
  • The Healing Tree: The tree signifies the cross of Christ; cast into the waters, it made them sweet ().
  • Jehovah Our Healer: The cross heals not only our circumstances but our sick self, in spirit, soul, and body ().

A Wilderness That Is Resurrection

Moses had asked Pharaoh for a three days' journey into the wilderness. After the sea, God took His people south, against natural geography, for exactly three days. To worldly friends, our new life after baptism looks like a wilderness; in God's eyes it is a realm of separation and resurrection, walked under the guidance of the cloud.

Goshen

The Old Realm

Plenty of Nile water and Egyptian leeks and onions, but no pillar of cloud.

Shur

The Realm of Resurrection

No natural supply, yet a new walk led by the Lord's presence ().

The Tree Cast into the Waters

Marah means bitterness, and it was the cloud that led them there. Instead of arguing with the murmuring people, Moses cried to the Lord, and the Lord showed him a tree. When we pray in a bitter situation, the Lord shows us the crucified Christ; as we apply the cross and live a crucified life, the bitterness turns sweet. This is not a single incident but a principle we meet again and again.

The Lord Who Heals You

Right after the waters were healed, the Lord tested the people and named Himself Jehovah their Healer. Only the sick need a physician (). The cross exposes our motives and shows that our mind, emotion, will, and even spirit are bitter and sick. Healing comes not through campaigns but as we cast the tree into each sick part, are subdued, and obey His voice.

Healed to Obey

The more we are healed, the more we have an ear to hear, a heart to keep His statutes, and a will to obey. Unhealed, our natural being remains a constitution of rebellion ().

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is the wilderness after the Red Sea a picture of resurrection?Three days and a new walk.

After Burial: Baptism buries us with Christ and raises us to walk in newness of life. The wilderness is the realm we enter on the other side.

Two Views: The world sees a barren place; God sees a realm separated from Egypt and led by His presence.

How do you usually respond at Marah: like the people or like Moses?Murmur or cry.

Murmuring: Complaining asks what kind of help this is, forgetting the same cloud saved us days before.

Crying: Turning to the Lord in prayer is what opens our eyes to the tree.

Why did the Lord speak of healing right after sweetening the waters?Bitterness within.

Situation and Self: Our circumstances are bitter, but so is our own being. The cross exposes both.

Testing: The experience of the cross proves us and shows where and what we are.

Where might you need to cast the tree today?Mind, emotion, will, spirit, relationships.

Specific Places: A bitter attitude toward someone, an unbalanced emotion, a will that decides wrongly. Each needs to be identified with Christ's crucifixion.

Daily Healer: Applied part by part, daily and even hourly, the crucified Christ becomes our Healer.
