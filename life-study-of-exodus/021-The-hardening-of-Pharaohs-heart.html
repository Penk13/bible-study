Exodus · Message 21

The Hardening of Pharaoh's Heart

Scripture says both that God hardened Pharaoh's heart and that Pharaoh hardened it himself. God's sovereignty and man's free will do not contradict; they correspond, and the result is a solemn warning.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Varied Expressions: God hardened his heart, Pharaoh hardened his heart, and his heart was hard; the variety shows how serious this is.
  • The Potter: As clay we have no ground to reply against God; He may make vessels of mercy and vessels of wrath (-).
  • Mercy by His Will: It is not of him who wills or runs, but of God who shows mercy ().
  • Free Will: God, in His greatness, wisdom, and love, created man able to choose, as with the two trees in Genesis 2.
  • Hardened, Then Hard: Once a heart is hardened it becomes hard, and God may leave it that way.

Three Undeniable Things

God's sovereignty and mercy are divine and eternal; man's free will is created by God. Moses illustrates mercy, Pharaoh illustrates hardening (), and both bear the weight of their own choices.

Worship

God's Sovereignty

He has the right to do as He wills. We do not argue; we bow.

Thank

God's Mercy

Shown to whom He wills (), not earned by our running.

Fulfill

Man's Free Will

God never forces; the choice and its responsibility are ours.

Who Took the First Step?

Our salvation was planned and initiated by God, yet we did the believing. In the same way God announced beforehand that He would harden Pharaoh's heart (), and Pharaoh carried it out by his own will (). Moses cannot boast, and Pharaoh has no excuse.

A Serious Warning

Before something negative toward the Lord develops, we still have two choices. Once it develops and the heart is hardened, only one choice remains, and God may not intervene to change the result.

Humble and Responsible

Humble yourself under God's sovereignty, take up your responsibility, and never let a negative thing toward Him take root. Then you will be a Moses, a vessel of mercy, not a Pharaoh.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

How can God's sovereignty and man's responsibility both be true?Your own salvation.

God Initiates: He planned and scheduled our believing; we were not seeking Him.

We Execute: Still, God did not believe for us. We believed. The two sides correspond rather than contradict.

What attitude shows that we are favored by God?Neither proud nor passive.

Not Proud: A zealous seeker confesses that even his seeking is of God's sovereignty.

Not Passive: Saying “It's all sovereign, so I need do nothing” is a sign of denying God. The more we bear our responsibility, the stronger the sign of His choosing.

Why is there little we can say about God's sovereignty except to worship?Clay and Potter.

No Explanation: We cannot say why He chose Jacob and not Esau, or why He showed us mercy.

Worship: Realizing we are clay ends our arguing and turns it into worship and thanks.

Is there anything negative toward the Lord beginning to grow in you?Two choices, then one.

Now: While it is small, you can still turn and keep your heart soft.

Later: Thinking “I'll change later” is dangerous; the result of a hardened heart may remain.
