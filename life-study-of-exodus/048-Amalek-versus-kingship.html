Exodus · Message 48

Amalek versus Kingship

The flesh is anything we do apart from grace, good or bad. As Ishmael it opposes grace; as Amalek it is a hand against God's throne. Sparing its best parts cost Saul his kingship and costs us our spiritual authority.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • A Definition: The flesh is whatever does not work by grace, even speaking Scripture or loving one's wife by the self.
  • Ishmael vs. Isaac: The issue of the flesh opposes the issue of grace, who typifies Christ; apart from Him we can do nothing ().
  • Against the Throne: The flesh cannot be subject to God (), so the kingdom (Exo. 18) comes only after Amalek is dealt with.
  • Kingship Supports Prayer: Obedience increases our appetite for prayer; rebellion kills it.
  • Saul's Loss: Sparing Agag and the best cattle was rebellion, like witchcraft and idolatry ().

Flesh versus Grace

God's promise to Abraham was pure grace: God would do everything. But at Sarah's suggestion Abraham used his own strength and produced Ishmael, and God did not appear to him for thirteen years. God waited until Abraham was as good as dead, then visited him with Isaac. Whatever we do without depending on God is an Ishmael, and it frustrates God's eternal purpose.

Flesh versus Kingship

Amalek is "a hand against the throne" (). In the flesh we act as if we had our own rights, and Satan hides behind it, as he spoke through Peter. Hur, of kingly Judah, upheld Moses' hand: subjection to God's authority keeps prayer alive. As kings and a royal priesthood (; ), we should carry the weight of kingship today.

How Saul Lost the Kingship

Saul was rightly anointed yet lost the throne through his handling of Amalek. Many saints lack spiritual weight for the same reason: their culture, opinions, peculiarities, and habits keep the "good" flesh alive.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

How can a prayer or a Bible message be "flesh"?Source, not subject.

Source: The flesh is defined by not depending on God, not by the content of what we do.

Result: Anything from ourselves produces an Ishmael that stands against Christ.

Why is the good flesh more hateful to God than the evil flesh?Best and choice.

Hidden: We condemn sin easily but treasure our natural virtues and even offer them to God.

Rebellion: Offering by our own choice defies His throne and gives ground to Agag.

How does my obedience affect my prayer life?Hur.

Disobedience: Rejecting the inner anointing can leave us with no desire to pray for days.

Subjection: Honoring His authority increases the appetite to pray, even while shopping.

Why do some long-time believers carry little spiritual authority?Unslain Agag.

Consumed: Peculiarities, opinions, and habits preserve the flesh and eat away the kingship.

Remedy: No excuses; let the light expose every natural thing and utterly destroy it.
