Exodus · Message 1

An Introductory Word

Exodus is not a repetition of Genesis but another line of experience: a book of pictures — redemption, supply, leading, revelation, and building — in which Christ is everything.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Progressive revelation: God spoke book by book over some 1,600 years, completing the word of God through Paul (). No one may add to or take away from it; our work is to read, study, and search it progressively.
  • Another line, not a continuation: Historically Exodus continues Genesis, but experientially it reveals a side of spiritual experience Genesis does not have — vague and abstract in Genesis becomes solid and substantial in Exodus.
  • What Genesis lacks: A clear picture of redemption (the Passover), the Lord's specific daily care (manna and living water), His substantial leading (the pillar), God's dwelling place (the tabernacle), and God's visible glory.
  • Individual vs. corporate: Genesis ends with an individual Israel (the transformed Jacob, ); Exodus ends with a corporate Israel — God's dwelling among two million redeemed people.
  • A book of pictures: The burning bush, the rod, the leprous hand, the water turned to blood — every story is a picture of our own experience.

One Experience, Two Lines: Genesis vs. Exodus

The Old Testament is precious because it gives vivid pictures of spiritual experience. Genesis paints one line; Exodus paints the other. Where Genesis is vague, Exodus is solid — every aspect of experience in this book is substantial, from the pillar of cloud and fire to the glory filling the tabernacle.

A Book of Pictures: The Four Signs of the Calling

Exodus is a picture book — watch it as heavenly television. In the calling of Moses, four signs portray God's dealings with our natural man:

🔥

The Burning Bush

Our natural man. Burning without being consumed shows that God will not use our natural man — He kindles it without consuming it.

🐍

The Rod to a Serpent

Anything other than God that we rely on is a serpent — even a husband, an education, a bank account. Cast it down; take it up again only by the tail, at God's command.

✋

The Leprous Hand

Our flesh is the embodiment of leprosy — in it dwells no good. If we touch ourselves, we become leprous.

🩸

River Water to Blood

The enjoyment of the world is death. The Nile's produce was refreshing, but poured on dry land it became blood.

The General Sketch: From Enslavement to God's Dwelling

The Central Thought: Christ for the Mutual Dwelling

Christ is seen throughout the book. He is the redemption, salvation, and supply of God's people, and the means for them to worship and serve God — so that in Him they may be built up with God to meet, communicate, and dwell mutually.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is Exodus not an experiential continuation of Genesis, and what side of experience does it add?Consider calling vs. redemption, vague vs. substantial, and where the patriarchs were actually redeemed.

Another line: Genesis portrays one line of the believer's experience — calling, inheritance, transformation. Exodus paints a different side: redemption, care, leading, revelation, and building — all solid and substantial.

The missing picture: In Genesis there is no clear record of redemption, not even for Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. They were redeemed where the Passover is portrayed. Without Exodus, our portrait of salvation and of God's dwelling is incomplete.

Which of the four signs' messages convicts you most, and why?The bush (natural man not used, but not consumed), the rod (relied-on things are the serpent), the hand (the flesh is leprosy), the water (worldly enjoyment is death).

The bush: God calls us by kindling our natural man without using it or consuming it. He does not intend to use our natural ability for His purpose.

The rod: Whatever we depend on other than God — education, career, savings, even a beloved person — becomes a serpent when we cast it down. We take it up again only to serve God's purpose.

The hand and the water: Touching ourselves brings leprosy; drinking the world brings death. These four signs together unmask our fallen condition and prepare us truly to be called.

Why is the corporate dimension of our experience "richer, higher, and greater" than the individual, even though it is basic?Compare the individual Israel at the end of Genesis with the corporate Israel at the end of Exodus ().

The seed and the tree: The individual Israel (Genesis 35) is like a sprout; the corporate Israel of Exodus 40 is the full-grown tree — the increase and enlargement of the individual. God's goal is a corporate vessel, His habitation, not isolated persons.

We were saved together: In God's eyes all believers were raised up and seated together in the heavenlies at the same time (). Redemption is corporate; only calling has an individual element ().

What is the central thought of Exodus, and what does it mean to be "built up with God" to meet and dwell mutually today?The tabernacle as the meeting place — Christ as every item of supply, worship, and building.

Christ is everything: As the Passover He redeems us; as manna and living water He supplies us; as the Red Sea He terminates us; as the tabernacle He opens the way to worship and serve God (; ).

Building, not leaving: Exodus does not end in freedom; it ends in God's dwelling filled with glory (). The goal of our entire journey is that God and man are built together in Christ to mutually meet, communicate, and dwell.
