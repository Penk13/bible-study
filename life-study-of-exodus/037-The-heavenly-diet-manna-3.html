Exodus · Message 37

The Heavenly Diet — Manna (3)

Manna was small, fine, round, white, like frost and coriander seed, solid, bright as bdellium, sweet and fragrant, and a mystery. Each feature shows how Christ, eaten as food, makes us balanced, pure, transparent, and eternal.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Small: Food must be small enough to eat. Christ fled from being made king and offered Himself as the bread of life (; ).
  • Fine, Round, White, Frost: Eating Christ makes us even, eternal and full, pure and simple, refreshed and cooled.
  • Coriander Seed & Solid: Christ enters as a tiny seed full of life; like hard manna, He must be ground, beaten, and boiled through our circumstances.
  • Bdellium, Oil, Honey: Manna looked like a transparent eye and tasted fragrant and sweet; eating Christ makes us transparent and lets us taste the Spirit.
  • A Mystery: "Manna" means "What is it?" The resurrected Christ, the life-giving Spirit with a body of flesh and bones, cannot be explained ().

The Smallness of Christ

We praise the Lord for His greatness, yet hardly sing of His smallness. Born in a manger and raised in a carpenter's home in a despised town, He would not display greatness. Corruption has always entered the church through the door of greatness, and the craving for great things breeds exaggeration and even fraud. Miracles are not food; the divine way is quiet and hidden, as with Nicodemus at night.

👑

Christ Made Great

Enthroned by the crowd, sought in spectacular revivals and miracles that do not last.

🍞

Christ Made Small

Bread small enough to take in, digest, and assimilate as our life supply.

What Eating Manna Produces

Each attribute of manna becomes, by eating, a quality in us. We are naturally coarse, unbalanced, mixed, and hot; Christ and His word transform us.

Seed, Solid, and Taste

As a coriander seed, Christ is tiny yet full of life, growing quietly. The Spirit too works in a small but unceasing way; those saved without excitement often go on most steadily. Manna was also solid: the people ground it, beat it, and boiled it before making cakes (). What we gather in the morning becomes edible through our daily situations and the help of experienced saints. Then Christ tastes of fresh oil (the Spirit's fragrance) and of honey (sweetness), never bitter.

Raw Manna

Reading the Bible without the millstones, mortars, and pots of experience leaves us with manna gathered but not yet food.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is Christ's smallness essential to His being our food?Food vs. a king.

Edible Size: We cannot take in what is too large; He made Himself small so we could eat Him.

His Choice: He refused the throne in John 6 and presented Himself as bread instead.

How can a desire for greatness harm the church?The open door.

Corruption Enters: Craving the spectacular invites boasting, exaggeration, and even fabricated miracles.

No Supply: Even a genuine miracle may not minister life; what does not nourish is not normal.

What does it mean to grind, beat, and boil Christ?Circumstances as tools.

Processing the Word: The word we gather is applied through situations and trials until it becomes our food.

With Others: Older saints help us turn raw manna into cakes.

How does eating Christ make us transparent?The eye of manna.

More Eyes: Manna looked like a bright eyeball; the more we eat, the more we see and receive light.

Our Appearance: His transparency becomes our color, consummating in the transparent New Jerusalem.
