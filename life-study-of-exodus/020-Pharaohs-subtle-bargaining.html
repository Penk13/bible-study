Exodus · Message 20

Pharaoh's Subtle Bargaining

God demanded a full exodus so that His people could feast to Him and sacrifice to Him. Pharaoh bargained in five subtle stages, but God insisted on His demand until not a hoof was left behind.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Many Pharaohs: Pharaoh signifies Satan, but also the self, the natural man, our natural mind, will, and emotion, and even relatives who argue against God's call.
  • Three Days' Journey: The distance into the wilderness signifies burial and resurrection, bringing God's people into a realm separated from Egypt ().
  • Feast and Sacrifice: Separation is not the goal; the goal is to enjoy God with God and to offer Christ to the Father ().
  • Five Bargains: Denial, worship in Egypt, not too far, only the men, and no flocks. After each, God answered not with argument but with another plague.
  • Plundering Egypt: The last plague made the Egyptians give silver, gold, and raiment, later used for the tabernacle ().

God's Demand

God did not ask merely that Israel leave Egypt. The third day in the Bible points to resurrection, and the wilderness, positively, is a realm of separation. Yet separation serves a further aim: that the people hold a feast to the Lord and present a sacrifice to Him.

Process

Buried & Raised

The natural man is buried so that God's people come out in resurrection.

Position

Separated

In the wilderness they are cut off from everything Egyptian and worldly.

Goal

Feast & Sacrifice

We enjoy God in His presence, and, touched by Christ's preciousness, we offer Him to the Father.

Five Stages of Bargaining

Many believers recognize their own story in these stages. Each concession sounds reasonable, even caring, but each one keeps a hold on God's people.

Moses' answer was that the cattle were needed not for living but for offering. His concern was God's need, not the people's.

God's Insistence

God never debates; He waits, then exercises His finger again. The slaughter of the firstborn finally forced Pharaoh to drive Israel out with flocks and herds. The Egyptians even gave their jewels, which cleared the account for years of unpaid labor and supplied the tabernacle.

Wasted on the Lord

Those thoroughly saved bring out of the world all they have and pour it on Christ, as Mary poured the costly ointment (). What the world calls waste becomes material for God's dwelling.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is separation not the final goal of God's demand?A feast to Him.

Means: Burial, resurrection, and separation bring us out of the world, but they only clear the ground.

End: God wants a people who enjoy Him in His presence and, out of that enjoyment, offer Christ to the Father for His satisfaction.

Which of Pharaoh's offers sounds most reasonable, and why is it dangerous?Balance and love.

Subtle Care: “Don't go too far” and “leave the little ones” sound like wisdom and concern.

Real Aim: Anything left in Egypt keeps the heart in Egypt, and Pharaoh can reach a people who stay close by.

How does God answer our reasoning with Him?The finger of God.

No Argument: God does not reason back. He is patient, sometimes for years, but He never withdraws His demand.

Another Plague: When our bargaining ends, He again touches our situation until we submit.

What in your life still remains in Egypt?Not a hoof left behind.

Possessions: Riches left in the world are a handle by which the enemy holds our heart.

Plunder: Instead of leaving things behind, we can bring them out and spend them on the Lord for His dwelling.
