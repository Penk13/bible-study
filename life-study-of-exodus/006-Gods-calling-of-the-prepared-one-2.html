Exodus · Message 6

God's Calling of the Prepared One (2)

God called Moses to bring His people out of Egypt and into a land flowing with milk and honey. The one He called had been stripped of self-confidence and saw himself as a thorn-bush, burning with God yet not consumed.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Out and In: Negatively, deliver Israel from Pharaoh's tyranny; positively, bring them into the good land (). Today this is the gospel, the power of God ().
  • Milk and Honey: Both come from animal and plant life combined, picturing Christ's redeeming life and generating life.
  • Terminated: Forty years in the wilderness took away Moses' confidence; at eighty he felt fit only for death ().
  • A Sent One: God is the Sender and Initiator; Moses only carries out His wish ().
  • The Burning Bush: God burns within and upon His called one, but never uses him as fuel.

Out of Egypt, Into the Good Land

Egypt is the kingdom of darkness and Pharaoh is Satan. Rescuing people from him is no small task. Yet the goal is not merely escape; it is Christ, the all-inclusive good land.

🐄

Milk and Honey

Cattle feed on grass; bees feed on flowers. Each product mingles animal and plant life.

✝️

The Riches of Christ

The Lamb who redeems and the loaf that supplies, enjoyed not as a taste in Egypt but as a spacious land.

The Called One

At forty Moses was "mighty in words" (); at eighty he said he was slow of speech (). Both are true. Educating a man is easy; terminating him takes forty years ().

At Forty

Burden + Natural Strength

Sure of his ability, he struck the Egyptian and fled when threatened.

At Eighty

Burden Without Self

Dealt with, without confidence, aware of his inability, and good only for death.

Divorce the Burden from Our Strength

We tend to carry God's burden with our own strength, and when that strength is dealt with, we drop the burden. God keeps the burden and sets aside our strength. A burden from Him cannot be laid down. Moses' reluctance angered God because it hid a secret complaint.

A Thorn-Bush Burning but Not Consumed

Thorns came with the curse; the first fire in the Bible kept fallen man from the tree of life (). Through redemption that same holy fire now burns within a cursed sinner. The Lord Himself later pointed back to "the bush" passage () as a lasting testimony.

The Bush

What We Are

A thorn-bush, not a cedar of Lebanon. Weariness in service may mean we are burning our own energy.

The Fire

What God Is

He burns with Himself as fuel and is glorified. Moses went to Pharaoh with only a rod, and God did everything.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is bringing people "into the good land" more than getting them out of Egypt?Deliverance needs a destination.

Negative Side: Freed from Satan's tyranny through the gospel.

Positive Side: Brought into Christ as a spacious land of milk and honey, enjoyed daily in the church life. Even young Moses was unclear about this goal.

Why does God deal with the natural strength of those He burdens?Keep the burden, lose the self.

Two Things Mixed: We marry God's burden to our ability. God wants to separate them.

His Way: "Do it, but by Me, not by yourself." Refusing to serve unless we can do it our own way hinders the Lord's work.

What is the difference between being a sender and a sent one?Who initiates?

The Sender: God alone initiates. "Send" appears again and again in Exodus 3 and 4.

The Sent One: Does nothing of himself, only the will of the One who sent him. This takes training, but it brings rest.

In your service, are you the fuel or the bush?Burning, not consumed.

Exhaustion as a Signal: If we are consumed, we may be serving with our natural energy.

Practice: Like Moses, who fell before God when opposed instead of arguing, let the vision of the bush remind you: "Lord, I am only a thorn-bush. You burn."
