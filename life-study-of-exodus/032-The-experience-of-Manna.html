Exodus · Message 32

The Experience of Manna

Even after the exciting springs of Elim, the flesh remains. It is exposed whenever we lack Christ as our daily life supply, and only the heavenly manna, eaten every morning, keeps it subdued.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Day and Night: After Elim the cloud led into the wilderness of Sin. Our experience needs both the bright days and the nights.
  • The Flesh Remains: The springs quench thirst but do not put the flesh to death; they even expose it ().
  • Hunger Exposes: Murmuring and lust come from being undernourished, short of Christ as the heavenly life supply.
  • Glory and Quails: God's glory appeared to rescue by condemning; quails satisfied lust but brought discipline ().
  • Morning, Dew, Camp: Manna comes in the morning, with the dew, around the camp ().

Elim Does Not Wash Away the Flesh

Exodus is written according to experience, not doctrine. Redeemed, healed at Marah, and refreshed at Elim, the people still murmured a month after the Passover. Even a genuine baptism in the Spirit is at most an Elim; it does not make us mature or eradicate the flesh. The flesh stays until our body is glorified, and it reappears whenever we are hungry.

💧

Springs at Elim

Occasional and exciting. They quench thirst, but hunger remains.

🍞

Manna Every Morning

Daily for forty years. Christ filling and satisfying us subdues the flesh and its lust ().

How God Dealt with the Flesh

At Marah God was not angry; here He was somewhat displeased; in Numbers 11 His fire burned. His murmuring people did not stop seeking Him, and the Lord heard every word. Two chapters, not one verse, are spent on manna, because manna is given only when certain conditions are met: the flesh exposed, our need recognized, discipline received.

Morning, Dew, and Camp

Quails came in the evening; manna came in the morning, giving a fresh start. It came with the dew, the Lord's gentle watering grace after the night, which is the base for the manna. There will be no dew in the New Jerusalem, only the river (), because there will be no night; today we need the dew. And it fell around the camp, nourishing God's people as an army.

No Storing Up

Yesterday's manna bred worms and stank (). The Christ we enjoyed yesterday is not enough for today.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why does a high spiritual experience not end our problem with the flesh?Thirst versus hunger.

Different Needs: Elim quenches thirst in the spirit, but the flesh is dealt with only when Christ fills and satisfies our hunger.

Exposure: The springs even expose the flesh. Claims that one blessing solves everything do not match experience.

What is usually behind our murmuring?Undernourishment.

Hunger: A negative attitude toward the church or the leading ones often comes from lacking Christ as our supply.

The Remedy: Not more complaint or more excitement, but eating Christ day by day.

How can the Lord's glory appear while we are complaining?Rescue by condemning.

Heard and Seen: The Lord hears every murmur. His appearing frightens us and stops our complaints.

For Our Rescue: The condemnation is not to slay us but to turn us back to Him.

Why might a morning time with the Lord yield no manna?Conditions and dew.

Conditions: Manna comes when the flesh has been exposed and we recognize our need for more than Elim.

With the Dew: We need His refreshing grace after the night, and we gather afresh each morning, not living on yesterday's supply.
