Exodus · Message 27

Israel's Exodus from Egypt (2)

Exodus 13 begins with sanctification and ends with God's presence as guide. Redeemed ones must be sanctified with Christ as their substitute, live an unleavened life worth remembering, and move in resurrection, where the Lord's leading is found.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Sanctified Firstborn: Whatever is redeemed must also be sanctified to the Lord ().
  • The Donkey and the Lamb: Unclean by nature, we need Christ as our substitute not only for redemption but also for sanctification ().
  • Days of Memorial: In the month of Abib, a new beginning of life without leaven makes each day worth remembering ().
  • Joseph's Bones: Carried out of Egypt into the good land, they signify that the exodus is only in resurrection ().
  • The Lord's Leading: He led by the pillar of cloud and of fire, not the short way but a roundabout one ().

Redeemed, Then Sanctified

Redemption is for our security; sanctification is for God's purpose (). Sheep and oxen could be offered directly, but the firstling of a donkey had to be redeemed again with a lamb or have its neck broken. As far as our nature goes, we are donkeys: even after redemption, the natural man is not acceptable on the altar.

Every Day a Memorial

Abib means sprouting: a new beginning of life. The way to remember the exodus was to eat unleavened bread, dealing with every sin as it is exposed. A day lived with Christ as our substitute, in new life and without tolerated leaven, is a day worth remembering.

Something to Remember

Any day that is not a memorial is a day of defeat. May we not come to the New Jerusalem with little to recall and many wasted days to regret.

Bones That Move in Resurrection

Joseph by faith made Israel swear to carry his bones up with them. Bone signifies unbreakable life; dry bones could reach Canaan only through resurrection. Flesh and blood, the natural man, cannot inherit the kingdom, but the risen Christ has flesh and bones (). Egypt was a cemetery; leaving it was a resurrection.

The Way of God's Leading

Right after Joseph's bones comes the pillar: God's leading is for bones that move. Chapter 12 gives the command; chapter 13 adds the leading, and a command without His leading is not enough. His guidance is conditional: sanctification, an unleavened new beginning, and resurrection. He chose the long way so the people would not turn back at the sight of war, and by such detours He trains us.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why did a redeemed donkey still need a lamb?Redemption vs. sanctification.

Still Unclean: Redemption spared it, but did not change its nature; it could not be offered for God's satisfaction.

Our Case: Offering our natural self on the altar is a serious mistake. Only Christ living in us is an acceptable sacrifice.

What makes a day of your Christian life a memorial?Abib and no leaven.

New Life: Taking Christ as our substitute gives a fresh, sprouting beginning.

Dealing with Sin: Not tolerating exposed sin keeps the day unleavened and worth remembering in eternity.

Why do sermons urging people to leave the world often fail?Joseph's bones.

Natural Effort: No one leaves the world by natural energy. Flesh and blood cannot do it.

Resurrection: Only the unbreakable divine life, coming forth in resurrection, moves dry bones out of Egypt.

How should we view the Lord's detours?Not straight paths.

Never Mistaken: The short way might have sent us back to Egypt. His leading, though it looks foolish, is always right.

Trained: Through roundabout ways we are educated and disciplined; we can praise Him for the detours.
