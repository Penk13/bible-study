Exodus · Message 46

The Defeat of Amalek

After manna and living water comes the first battle: Amalek, the flesh. Weak men fight, but victory depends on the interceding Christ in heaven and the indwelling Spirit who slays the flesh.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Supplied to Fight: Only those fed with manna and refreshed with living water can fight the flesh; the hungry are already subdued.
  • Amalek Is the Flesh: Warlike, destructive, and disturbing, a descendant of Esau close to Jacob, warring against the soul ().
  • The First Enemy: The flesh leads sin, the world, and Satan in keeping us from Christ as the good land.
  • Moses and Joshua: Moses' raised hand is Christ interceding (); Joshua is the Spirit fighting within ().
  • Destiny: The flesh is a hand against God's throne and will be blotted out; the altar, Jehovah-nissi, is the cross as our memorial of victory.

Why Amalek Attacks

Israel did not fight in Egypt or at the Red Sea; their first battle came only after they enjoyed manna and water from the rock. Before we enjoyed Christ we were unaware of the flesh, for we were simply its victims. When we rise up to eat and drink Christ, Satan stirs up the envious flesh to attack, often right after our morning time with the Lord. Its aim is to keep us from the full enjoyment of Christ.

Warlike

Loves Fighting

The name Amalek means warlike; the flesh never wants peace.

Destroying

Ruins What Is Good

It wrecks married life, family life, and church life.

Disturbing

Wretched Man

Paul's cry in shows how it troubles us.

Close to Jacob

Of the First Man

From Esau, twin of Jacob: the flesh lives right next to our regenerated being.

How Amalek Is Defeated

Joshua chose "men," a word implying weakness: God uses the weak. Israel prevailed when Moses' hand was up and failed when it dropped (). We must fight, yet our fighting counts only as we fight with Christ. Manna, water, Moses, and Joshua all typify Christ: He is all.

Moses on the Mountain

The ascended Christ interceding for us in heaven ().

Joshua in the Valley

The Destiny of Amalek

The flesh is not just a source of small wrongs; it is "a hand against the throne" of God, opposing His government and even His church. So God will wage war against it from generation to generation () and blot out its remembrance (), fully in the kingdom age.

Jehovah-nissi

Moses' altar signifies the cross that crucified the flesh (). The memory of the flesh is erased; the cross remains as the memorial and boast of our victory.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why does the battle with Amalek come after manna and water?Support and envy.

Support: Without the daily supply of Christ and the Spirit we have no strength; we are already one with the flesh.

Provocation: A radiant, satisfied people stirs the flesh's envy; enjoying Christ brings the attack into view.

Why is the flesh the foremost of our enemies?Sin, world, Satan.

Others Depend on It: When the flesh is put to death, the world cannot hold us, sin cannot work, and Satan is powerless.

Its Aim: To keep us from entering into the all-inclusive Christ as our land.

If victory depends on Moses' hand, why must we fight at all?Cooperate.

Not by Us: In ourselves we are weak men, qualified only for defeat.

With Him: Christ intercedes above and fights within; He wants us to fight together with Him.

What does it mean that the flesh is a hand against God's throne?Rebellion.

Rebel: The flesh opposes God's administration and plan, cannot obey Him, and even opposes the church.

God's Response: This is why He wars against it and will blot it out.

How is the cross both an altar and a banner for us?Jehovah-nissi.

Altar: The cross deals with the flesh; there it was crucified.

Banner: By the cross we enjoy the Lord as our victory, so it is the memorial we boast in.
