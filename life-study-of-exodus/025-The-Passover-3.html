Exodus · Message 25

The Passover (3)

The one-day Passover continues as a seven-day feast of unleavened bread: a whole life of enjoying Christ, dealing with exposed sin, eating instead of working, and partaking as purchased and circumcised ones rather than strangers or hired servants.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Seven Days: The feast of unleavened bread continues the Passover for a full course of time, signifying our whole life after salvation ().
  • No Leaven Seen: We cannot be sinless, but we must deal with every sin once it is exposed ().
  • Eat, Do Not Work: On the first and seventh days nothing was allowed but eating; God's salvation leaves no room for our doing ().
  • Who May Eat: Not strangers or hired servants, but servants bought with money and circumcised ().
  • Judgment: God struck the firstborn and judged the gods of Egypt; the old man and Satan have no part in the feast ().

A Feast That Lasts a Lifetime

The Bible stresses eating the Passover, for we live by what we eat. Leaven, which softens dough and makes it easy to take in, signifies sin. Christ is both the lamb and the unleavened bread; the more we eat His sinless life, the more unleavened we become. Paul applies this directly: Christ our Passover has been sacrificed, so purge out the old leaven and keep the feast ().

Unleavened Bread

The joy continued through the whole course of our life by dealing with exposed sin.

Only Eating Is Allowed

Many believers lose their early joy because they tolerate leaven that has been exposed; tolerating sin costs enjoyment and fellowship. Equally, many replace eating with working: resolutions to be humble, patient, or even to pray more can become a work God does not allow.

Invited to a Feast

A host who invites you to dinner is not pleased if you spend the evening mowing his lawn. He wants you at the table. Drop the doing, but keep the eating.

Who Is Qualified to Eat

Strangers picture both unbelievers and our natural man; hired servants work for wages. Grace is not of works (), and we are sons, not servants (). Those who eat are bought back and have had the natural life cut off by the cross ().

What the Passover Excludes and Includes

The firstborn stand for everyone in Adam, the first man; the stranger and the firstborn are two names for the old, natural life. On the same night God also judged the gods of Egypt, the demons behind Satan's kingdom. What remains is a people brought out by their armies ().

No Place

Excluded

Leaven, work, strangers, hired servants, the firstborn, Egypt's gods: sin, human doing, the natural life, the old man, the world, and Satan.

Components

Included

The lamb, the house, unleavened bread, bitter herbs, hyssop, girdle, shoes, and staff, issuing in God's army.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why does the joy of salvation fade for many believers?One day vs. seven.

No Continuation: They had the Passover but not the feast of unleavened bread; exposed sin was left in the house.

The Remedy: Deal with each sin as it is exposed and keep eating Christ as the unleavened bread; enjoyment returns.

How can even prayer become a forbidden work?Resolutions.

Natural Effort: When we resolve in ourselves to pray more, prayer becomes our doing rather than our enjoyment.

Eat Instead: God's salvation permits only eating; prayer should come from feeding on Christ, not from human resolve.

Do you live as a hired one or a purchased one?Wages vs. bought.

Hired: Serves for compensation and may resign when unhappy. There is no grace or enjoyment in that position.

Purchased: Bought back at a price, a slave of Christ like Paul, with no right to resign, and freely enjoying the feast.

Why did God judge only the firstborn?Adam and Christ.

In Adam: Adam was the first man, so the firstborn include all that is of Adam. Christ is the second man.

In Experience: The blood redeems nothing of the old man; we must condemn the firstborn and the stranger within to apply the Passover fully.
