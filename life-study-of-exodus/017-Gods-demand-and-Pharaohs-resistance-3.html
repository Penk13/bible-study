Exodus · Message 17

God's Demand and Pharaoh's Resistance (3)

God wants a people who have nothing but Himself. The plagues of frogs and lice unveil, to the Egyptians and to God's people, that the world's water breeds trouble and its dust breeds irritation.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Nothing but God: To be saved is to be brought out of every usurpation to the mountain of God, where He is our center, life, and home.
  • A Picture of the World: The New Testament says the world lies in the evil one (); Exodus shows what that world looks like.
  • Frogs: The waters that once gave fish now gave frogs, a nuisance in every bed and oven. Every worldly gain becomes a "frog."
  • Lice: The dust that once gave grain now gave lice (). The magicians failed and confessed, "the finger of God" ().
  • Death, Trouble, Irritation: The first three plagues unveil the nature, meaning, and result of living in the world.

Unveiling Egypt to God's People

The plagues were not only warnings and punishments to Egypt; they were a revelation to Israel. Remembering the leeks and onions () later troubled them concerning God's dwelling. Only those who loathe Egypt can be built up as His house.

Egypt

Mount Horeb

Light, revelation, purpose, God's presence, and a future full of His supply. Separated people become His dwelling.

Fish to Frogs, Grain to Lice

God did not destroy Egypt with one powerful blow. In wisdom and mercy He used small things, again and again, to remind His people that Egypt is not lovable.

Bringing Frogs and Taking Them Away

The magicians could bring up frogs () but could not remove them; at Moses' cry the Lord did. A true evangelist does both: he makes people see the world's enjoyments as frogs, and through prayer the frogs are taken away.

Relief Without Repentance

When Pharaoh saw relief, he hardened his heart (). Some, once delivered from trouble, likewise refuse to believe.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What does it mean to be saved, according to Exodus?More than escaping punishment.

Brought Out: God rescues His people from everything other than Himself, from Satan's usurpation and Egypt's preoccupation.

Brought To: At Horeb God was their center, business, life, and home. Salvation brings us to a place where there is nothing but God.

Which of your "fish" have turned out to be "frogs"?Everything gathered from the Nile.

The Pattern: What we gather from the world's supply, possessions or even dear relationships held apart from God, eventually becomes a source of trouble.

The Point: Not to make us bitter, but to show that the world's enjoyment is not true enjoyment.

Why would God use frogs and lice instead of one decisive blow?Wisdom and mercy.

Instruction: One blow gives no warning or revelation. Repeated small exposures teach, step by step.

For His People: Each plague helped Israel see Egypt clearly so they would want to leave it.

Is the same plague a punishment or a revelation?It depends on the response.

Received: To one who accepts mercy, it is an unveiling that Egypt is a land of frogs.

Refused: To one who rejects mercy, it is simply punishment to be endured, as with Pharaoh.
