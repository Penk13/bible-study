Exodus · Message 47

The Continual Fighting against Amalek

The flesh is the last enemy keeping the kingdom and God's building from coming in. We fight it by praying in union with the interceding Christ, by slaying it with the indwelling Spirit, by sparing nothing of it, and by obeying the Lord.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Two Sections: Exodus 1–17 deals with the world and the flesh; chapters 18–40 bring in the kingdom and the building of the tabernacle.
  • Enmity against God: The flesh, our whole fallen being, is not subject to God and cannot please Him (; ).
  • Prayer Upheld: Moses' heavy hands, held up by Aaron and Hur while he sat on a stone, picture our prayer joined to Christ's intercession.
  • Slay with the Spirit: Based on the old man's crucifixion (), we put the flesh to death and the Spirit works with us.
  • Spare Nothing: Saul kept the best of Amalek; obedience is better than sacrifice ().

No Kingdom without Dealing with the Flesh

Pharaoh, Egypt, and Amalek picture Satan, the world, and the flesh. Once these are dealt with, the kingdom comes, and in the kingdom God's house is built, as Solomon built the temple after David's victories. The divisions among Christians are mainly due to the flesh. God therefore wars with Amalek in every generation, even to Haman the Agagite in Esther, the hidden flesh working to destroy God's people.

Praying with the Interceding Christ

Christ's hands never grow heavy, yet Moses' did, so the picture speaks of our prayer. While Christ intercedes above, we pray on earth in union with Him. When prayer stops, the flesh prevails (), for even after decades of experience the flesh never improves.

Slaying the Flesh Thoroughly

Joshua in the valley is the Spirit fighting within (). Because the old man was crucified, we boldly crucify the flesh (); as soon as we cooperate, the Spirit does the work. Today the word to obey is the inner anointing ().

🐑

Saul's Partial Obedience

He destroyed the vile but spared Agag and the best sheep and oxen. Their bleating exposed him.

🗡️

Utter Destruction

Every part of the flesh, bad or seemingly good, put to death; our "better" points included.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why must the flesh be dealt with before God's house can be built?Kingdom first.

Order: The kingdom comes after the enemies are dealt with, and the building follows the kingdom.

Today: Where the flesh prevails, Christ's headship is absent, and the church cannot be built.

Why do my praying hands grow heavy?Aaron and Hur.

Spirit: A shortage of priesthood leaves the spirit dull; deal with the spirit rather than forcing more prayer.

Authority and Goal: Disobedience and a lack of care for God's building also weigh our prayer down.

How does admitting weakness become a solid base for prayer?The stone.

Honesty: We cannot pray until sunset by ourselves.

Resting: Telling the Lord so, and taking Him as our base, gives us a seat to keep praying.

What are the "bleating sheep" in my life?Good flesh.

Choice Flesh: Points in which we feel better than others, kept under the excuse of serving God.

God's Demand: All of the flesh, good or bad, must go; obedience matters more than sacrifice.
