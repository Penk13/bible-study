Exodus · Message 33

God's Dealing with the Flesh of His People

God had changed His people's location; with manna He set out to change their constitution. He deals with the flesh not by cutting it into pieces but by changing the diet, replacing the Egyptian element with a heavenly one.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Everything for Us: In His salvation God wants to be everything to us and to do everything for us, as He did against Pharaoh.
  • Thirty-One Days: So soon after many miracles, the people lusted for Egypt's fleshpots, as if they had seen nothing ().
  • A Heavenly Diet: Quails belong to the old creation; manna came from heaven and belongs to no earthly category ().
  • Quails in Discipline: Lust satisfied, then loathed, then judged at the graves of lust ().
  • The Real Dealing: The flesh is set aside by not being fed. A changed diet reconstitutes God's people.

A Change of Constitution

Chapter sixteen is in a sense greater than the Passover or the Red Sea, for here God's people begin to be transformed with a heavenly element. We become what we eat. Egyptian food had built Egypt into them; out of Egypt in name, they were still Egyptian in nature. For forty years God fed them with heavenly food to make them a heavenly people.

Positional

Out of Egypt

A change of place: outward and objective, but the constitution unchanged ().

Dispositional

Egypt Out of Them

A change of diet: a heavenly metabolism replacing the Egyptian element with the heavenly ().

Beyond a Test of Faith

A natural reading sees only God testing their unbelief. With light, we see that the redeemed people still wanted to live the old life, while God wanted them to live a heavenly one. In wisdom He delayed the food until they felt the need, so the change of diet would leave a lasting impression. If they had simply rested in Him, there would have been no quails, only manna in the morning.

Glory, Sufficiency, and the Graves of Lust

The flesh here is the whole fallen being, the old person. If we keep lusting after worldly things, God may give them in abundance, not as approval but to show His glory, anger, and sufficiency; often spiritual death follows. His glory is felt most sharply when we disobey. Eventually we learn to want no more quails and to be content with the simple manna.

Set Aside, Not Cut Up

Flesh cut into pieces is still flesh. God deals with it by sending food the flesh does not like and feeding His people from heaven day by day.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What is the difference between a change of location and a change of constitution?Out of Egypt vs. Egypt out of us.

Location: Being saved and separated from the world is real but outward.

Constitution: God's goal is to change our inner fabric by what we eat, making us heavenly in nature, not merely in name.

Why is getting what we lust after not a sign of God's approval?Quails.

Given in Anger: The quails came in abundance, then became loathsome, then brought a plague.

Spiritual Death: Many believers have received the cars, money, or houses they craved and lost the freshness of life.

How should God's people respond when a need arises?A real, faithful, purposeful God.

Rest and Praise: He knows our need. If He withholds for a time, we can fast with rejoicing and wait for His supply.

No Quails Needed: Such faith would have led straight to the manna.

Why is changing the diet a deeper way to deal with the flesh?Fed or starved.

Not Feeding It: Egyptian food nourishes the flesh. Heavenly food does not suit it and so sets it aside.

Reconstitution: As we daily eat Christ, the heavenly element gradually replaces the old, and we are transformed.
