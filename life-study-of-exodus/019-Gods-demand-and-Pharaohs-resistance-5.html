Exodus · Message 19

God's Demand and Pharaoh's Resistance (5)

In the third group of plagues God changed the very functions of the universe: rain became hail, wind brought locusts, and the sun gave way to darkness. From blood to darkness, the world goes from death to death.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Heavens for the Earth: God ordained sunshine, wind, and rain to sustain life on earth for man, and man for God.
  • Functions Changed: Rain became hail mingled with fire (), wind brought locusts, and a darkness that could be felt stopped all movement ().
  • Not a Hoof Left: Pharaoh bargained to keep the children, then the flocks; Moses refused every compromise ().
  • Made to Stand: God upheld stubborn Pharaoh to show His power and declare His name ().
  • Worldliness: Friendship with the world is enmity with God (); it offends His holiness, higher than His righteousness.

The Universe Turned Against Egypt

The first two groups exposed water, earth, and air. The third altered the laws that govern them, so that Egypt's very atmosphere was no longer fit for human life.

Spiritually, a proper relationship with the Lord brings rain to our spirit and Christ as a gentle breeze; rebellion turns the rain to hail.

From Death to Death

The plagues began with blood and ended, before the firstborn, with darkness; both signify death. God's creation in Genesis moves from life to life, but the world moves from death to death, with frogs, lice, flies, pestilence, boils, hail, and locusts in between.

Sinfulness

Against Righteousness

Many Christians are careful about temper and sin.

Worldliness

Against Holiness

Few hate the world, yet loving it makes one an enemy of God ().

Separated for His Dwelling

Full redemption includes both the Passover and the Red Sea: Christ gave Himself to save us from sins and to deliver us out of this present evil age (). Only a people separated from the world can become God's dwelling place.

No Exhortation Needed

Once we see that the world's water is blood and its dust is lice, we stop loving it spontaneously.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What do Pharaoh's bargains reveal about the enemy's strategy?The children, then the flocks.

Partial Release: Pharaoh would let the men go if the little ones stayed, knowing parents' hearts would remain with them; later he would keep the herds.

Full Exodus: Moses insisted on young and old, flocks and herds. What is left in Egypt keeps a hold on us and robs God of offerings.

Why would God make a stubborn Pharaoh stand?Education for the generations.

His Name: Pharaoh's resistance gave occasion to display God's power throughout the earth.

Our Education: Without fourteen chapters of conflict we would not know the world so thoroughly, nor God's attitude toward it.

Why is worldliness more serious than we think?Righteousness and holiness.

Sin: Losing our temper offends God's righteousness.

The World: Loving the world offends His holiness and makes us His enemy, yet we may love it unknowingly, mistaking blood for water.

How has the Lord been rain and breeze to your spirit?The spiritual functions.

Normal: In a proper relationship He waters the garden of our spirit and refreshes us like a gentle breeze.

Altered: Stubbornness can turn the same functions into hail and fire. Keeping our relationship right keeps the heavens open.
