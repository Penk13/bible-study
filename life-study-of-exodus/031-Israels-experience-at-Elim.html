Exodus · Message 31

Israel's Experience at Elim

Elim, with its twelve springs and seventy palm trees, pictures resurrection life issuing from the cross: life flowing out of God into us and growing up within us, equipping God's people as an army to carry out His ministry.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • A Downward Way: The cloud led south, not north. Resurrection lifts us, but we experience it by being led into lower places.
  • From Marah: Elim issues from Marah; the experience of resurrection follows the experience of the cross.
  • Springs and Palms: Water flowing down and trees growing up: divine life flowing into us and growing within us ().
  • Twelve and Seventy: Numbers for God's people carrying out His ministry, eternally and dispensationally ().
  • Encamped as an Army: Supplied by flowing and growing life, the people were qualified to fight for God's purpose.

Resurrection by a Downward Way

The good land was higher than Egypt, yet the way there went down. We expect that after the cross we will rise; instead the Lord often leads us lower, into harder situations. We should not be frightened. When we are low, even in a tomb, we sense our need of resurrection, and following the cloud downward brings us to Elim. The word Elim means the mighty ones and is also linked to a grove of palms.

🌳

Marah: One Tree Cut Down

The cross heals bitterness, but there is no plantation and no growth there.

🌴

Elim: A Grove Growing

Springs from God's creation water His plantation of palms ().

The Meaning of the Numbers

The springs are rivers of living water flowing from within believers, the Spirit as the divine life in resurrection (). The palms speak of life that flourishes (), rejoices in satisfaction, and overcomes tribulation (). Twelve and seventy appear together when God's people carry out His ministry: the twelve tribes and seventy elders, the twelve apostles and the seventy sent out.

Encamped by the Waters

"Encamped" shows that the people were being formed into an army. At the Red Sea God fought for them; after Marah and Elim, they could fight themselves, as they do in chapter seventeen, for the goal of God's habitation. Many seekers since the mystics have lingered at Marah, testifying of bitterness made sweet. The Lord wants us to go on from the healing tree to the flowing springs and growing palms.

Not Settled but Encamped

Eating the Passover is not enough for the battle. We must pass through Marah to Elim, where sweet water becomes flowing water and we grow like palms to express God's rich life and full victory.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is the experience of resurrection found on a downward path?Where we need it.

Need Reveals It: In the heavenlies we sense no need for resurrection; in a low place, even in death, we do.

Following the Cloud: God's way is not our concept. Trusting His leading downward brings us to Elim.

What is the difference between Marah and Elim in our experience?One tree versus a plantation.

Marah: The cross changes bitterness into sweetness. It heals but does not produce growth.

Elim: Resurrection life flows in and grows up, expressing the riches and victory of the divine life.

How can a focus on the cross alone become a place to linger?Stopping short.

Good but Incomplete: The subjective cross was rightly recovered, yet testimony only of healed bitterness stops at Marah.

Going On: The Lord's present move is to water His plantation so that His people grow and are built up as an army.

What does it mean for a local church to be encamped at Elim?An army supplied.

Supplied: The saints enjoy the Spirit flowing and the life growing, not merely doctrines about it.

Ready to Fight: Such a people are no longer merely settled; they are equipped to fight for God's building.
