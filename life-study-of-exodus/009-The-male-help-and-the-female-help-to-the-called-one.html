Exodus · Message 9

The Male Help and the Female Help to the Called One

Moses' calling was not complete until he had Aaron and Zipporah. Every called one needs the male help, a matching partner according to the principle of the Body, and the female help, a cutting that circumcises the flesh.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • A Sweet Anger: When Moses still asked God to send someone else, the Lord's anger was kindled (), an intimate anger that opened His heart about Aaron.
  • Two as a Testimony: The Lord sent disciples two by two (); Paul wrote with Sosthenes (). Being individualistic violates the Body.
  • No Lost Position: Aaron would be Moses' mouth, and Moses would be as God to him ().
  • Zipporah's Cutting: On the way, God sought to kill Moses because his son was uncircumcised; Zipporah cut with a sharp stone ().
  • Both Needed: Matching is objective; cutting is subjective. After both, Moses' calling was complete.

Aaron: The Matching

The Lord had already prepared Aaron, but He waited until Moses felt his need. The match was hard: the younger brother led the elder, and later Aaron and Miriam spoke against Moses. Yet that friction kept Moses humble, "very humble, more than all men" ().

Individualistic Work

Hired Helpers

Gifted workers hire and fire employees. Much may be done, but little builds the Body.

The Body Principle

Matching Co-workers

A three-legged race: bound to another, restricted, protected. Moses did not hire Aaron; Paul did not hire Timothy.

Zipporah: The Cutting

Through Moses' weakness and Zipporah's opposition, the son had not been circumcised, and God met Moses at the inn to kill him (). Uncircumcision would end his part in God's ministry; circumcision ushers one into it.

Male Help and Female Help

God has already prepared both for each of His called ones; we need not choose them.

Male Help

Aaron Matching

Objective. Carries out God's commission together. Hard to bear, but a safeguard.

Female Help

Zipporah Cutting

Subjective. Cuts off the flesh and natural life. Even dear brothers may cut us while seeming to agree.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why did the Lord wait for Moses to feel his need before mentioning Aaron?Wisdom in fellowship.

God's Way: He often will not act for us until we realize our need. Only then could Moses accept so difficult a match.

Among Us: Even when we see another's need, it is often better to wait until he sees it himself.

How is a matching co-worker different from a hired helper?The Body versus the individual.

Hired: Serves the leader's work and can be dismissed. The leader remains individualistic.

Matched: Bound together as one unit, representing the Body. This is the only way to do the work of building.

Why did God seek to kill the very man He had just sent?Circumcision and ministry.

No Flesh in the Ministry: God cannot use one still living in the flesh or natural life. Without circumcision there is no part in the covenant or the ministry.

Negligence Exposed: Moses' yielding to his wife was an offense the Lord would not tolerate on the way to His commission.

Who are the Aarons and Zipporahs in your life?Thank Him for both.

Receive Them: The matching ones restrict us; the cutting ones make us "bloody husbands," under death. Both are His sovereign provision.

Pray: "Lord, I thank You for the matching and for the cutting. Keep me from being individualistic or uncircumcised."
