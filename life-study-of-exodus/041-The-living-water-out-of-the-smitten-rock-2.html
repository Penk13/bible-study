Exodus · Message 41

The Living Water out of the Smitten Rock (2)

God led His people to a dry place to test and expose them. At Massah all failed except God, yet He still smote His Christ so that living water could flow to a sinful, faithless people.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Acts, Not Ways: Israel had seen God's acts but did not know His ways (), so in the dry place they quarreled even beside the pillar.
  • Massah and Meribah: Testing and strife. Israel tested God, God tested Israel and Moses, and only God passed ().
  • Smitten Twice: At Kadesh Moses struck the rock he was told to speak to (), violating God's economy, and was barred from the land.
  • Exposed by Dryness: In church and family, shortage of Christ as living water turns us quarrelsome.
  • Eating in Drinking: Eating is in drinking, and drinking is in breathing; we have all been made to drink one Spirit ().

A Photograph of Us

After plagues, Passover, Red Sea, Marah, Elim, and manna, Israel came to a waterless place and quarreled with Moses. We would likely do the same. Their pillar was outside; ours is within, and often while complaining, even about the elders, we sense the Lord who brought us there. Knowing God's ways, they could have praised Him, confident that He who led them would supply them.

Passed

God

Massah and Kadesh

At the start of the journey God bore with failure. Thirty-eight years later, the same complaint drew His severe dealing, because Moses touched God's administration. Christ, the rock, was to be smitten once; afterward one need only speak to Him. Smiting again is, in effect, crucifying Christ anew.

Waters of Strife, Water of Peace

No church stays at Elim. God brings us to Massah and stays silent to expose us; with plenty to drink we are gentle, in drought we fight. The water meant for peace is named Meribah because of our failure. Still God did not withhold it: He used the law to smite His Christ for sinners. For the water to flow out of us, we too must be identified with the smitten Christ.

Drinking Comes First

The river proceeds from the throne and the tree grows in it (). Without the water of life there is no spiritual food, and without breathing there is no practical drinking.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What is the difference between knowing God's acts and His ways?Psalm 103:7.

Acts: Israel saw miracles yet panicked at the first shortage.

Ways: Knowing that God initiated their journey and was present would have turned complaint into praise.

Why does God lead us into dry places?Exposure.

To Test: His silence shows whether we will pray and thank or murmur and quarrel.

To Expose: Shortage reveals our sinfulness and our need of Christ as living water.

Why was striking the rock twice so serious?God's administration.

Once for All: Christ's death is not to be repeated; now we speak to the smitten rock.

Beyond Personal: Moses' reaction touched a basic principle of redemption, so God dealt with him strictly.

How are eating, drinking, and breathing related?One within another.

Contained: Food is in the water, and drinking happens by breathing; John 6 leads into John 7.

Practice: Spiritual breathing gives us a practical way to drink the Spirit, and in drinking we are fed.
