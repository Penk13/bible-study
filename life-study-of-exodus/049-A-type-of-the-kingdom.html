Exodus · Message 49

A Type of the Kingdom

Exodus 18 happened later in history but is placed here by doctrine. Between salvation and building stands the kingdom: once the flesh is defeated, the Gentiles turn to God, the church prevails, and order comes under Christ's headship.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Doctrine over Sequence: The events occurred after the tabernacle was built () but were inserted here to complete the picture.
  • Kingdom after the Flesh: When the Spirit with the cross subdues the flesh, the kingdom is present ().
  • Jethro: A Midianite priest of the true God, picturing godly Gentiles who come to worship ().
  • Zipporah: Moses' Gentile wife, gained while he was rejected, typifying the church.
  • Captains: Rulers of thousands down to tens keep order under Moses as head ().

Why Chapter 18 Is Here

Like Matthew, Exodus arranges events by meaning rather than by date. Its focus is not mainly the giving of the law but God's full salvation for building His dwelling. Without the kingdom, salvation has no proper issue; a believer may be scriptural, ethical, and humble, yet still wholly in the flesh. Galatians 5 links four words: flesh, Spirit, cross, and kingdom ().

Four Features of the Kingdom

Exodus gives pictures, not definitions. Paul himself treated its items as types, so Jethro and Zipporah need not be exceptions. Applied today: when we preach in the Spirit, with the cross killing the flesh, people turn to God, and the gospel becomes the gospel of the kingdom ().

Captains, Not Only Elders

Some regard Jethro's advice as human organization overturned in Numbers 11. But the two accounts differ: Exodus 18 and Deuteronomy 1 deal with daily burdens and strife through captains, while Numbers 11 answers a rebellion against God through seventy elders who prophesied. The second strengthens rather than cancels the first.

A Sign of the Kingdom

If every small matter must go to the elders, the church is weak. Where the kingdom is real, a brother or sister nearby can point others to the Spirit and the cross, and order is kept under Christ's headship.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is Exodus not mainly a book about the law?First and last chapter.

Start and Finish: It opens with God's people in bondage and ends with God's dwelling among them.

Focus: The law is there, but the goal is salvation unto the building of the tabernacle.

How do I know the kingdom is present in my daily life?Hard to offend.

Flesh Subdued: The Spirit with the operating cross stops the negative word before it is spoken.

Peace: In the Spirit we are hard to offend, and home and church life are peaceful.

Why does much gospel work see few people turn?Jethro comes later.

In the Flesh: Hard work in the flesh, even pleasant and kind, does not bring people to God.

Amalek First: When the flesh is defeated, Jethro comes and the church prevails.

What does it mean to be a "captain" in the church?Order, not control.

Not Control: No one controls others; all are under the living Spirit and the cross.

Function: Under Christ's headship, one can help settle problems locally so that order is kept.
