Exodus · Message 28

Pharaoh's Last Struggle

God used Pharaoh's opposition to bring about the Passover, the exodus, and finally the baptism of His people in the Red Sea, where Satan and the world were buried and Pharaoh's usefulness ended.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Three Aspects: Passover is redemption, the exodus is leaving the world, and the Red Sea crossing is baptism; Pharaoh supplied the environment for all three.
  • A Tempting Detour: Israel's turn toward the sea looked like aimless wandering and lured Pharaoh to pursue ().
  • Hardened for God's Glory: God hardened Pharaoh's heart to follow, using even the enemy for His purpose ().
  • Light and Darkness: The Angel of God and the pillar moved behind Israel, light to them and darkness to the Egyptians ().
  • Buried in the Sea: Not one of Pharaoh's army remained; Satan and the world are buried in baptism ().

Pharaoh, Used by God

Pharaoh was not the source of salvation, but without his resistance there would have been no setting for it. The more he struggled, the more he helped. God raised him up to show His power (), just as He still lets Satan serve His purpose. Moses' patience with Pharaoh also rebukes our quickness to give up on people.

Seemingly a Dead End

Hemmed in between the army and the sea, the people complained eloquently. Moses did not argue; he told them to fear not, stand still, and see the Lord's salvation, and the Lord then told them to go forward. New believers rarely go straight from conversion to baptism; God's detours stir up opposition that makes their baptism thorough.

Bring the Army into the Water

When relatives and friends persecute a new convert, there is much to bury. Baptism then buries not only the believer's old life but the army of the world with it.

The Guiding Light Becomes a Protecting Light

The Angel of God is the Angel of Jehovah of chapter 3, Christ as God's sent One, and He and the pillar moved together. The light that guides those who follow the Lord protects them from turning back; to opposers it becomes darkness, until they lose even their reason, as with the religionists the Lord called blind ().

Before Us

Guiding Light

Received from the time we first believed, showing the way.

Behind Us

Protecting Light

Under attack, it confirms our way and stands between us and the opposers.

Terminated and Buried

The Lord confused the Egyptians and took off their chariot wheels; they wanted to flee, but it was too late. Once Pharaoh had served God's purpose, he was buried and never struggled again (). Opposition that remains in our lives must still be needed for our perfecting; when it is no longer useful, the Lord will bury it too.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

How can opposition serve God's salvation in your life?Pharaoh's three helps.

Environment: Pharaoh's resistance created the setting for the Passover, the exodus, and the baptism.

Our View: Rather than resent opposers, we can see them as useful in God's hand for perfecting us, for as long as He needs them.

Why should we not expect a straight path from conversion to baptism?The roundabout way.

God's Leading: He led Israel to the sea, not directly to Canaan. New believers may also face detours that look foolish to the world.

Thorough Baptism: The persecution these detours stir up gives baptism real content: the world's army is buried with them.

What did Moses do when the people complained?Stand still, then go forward.

No Argument: He did not fight the people but pointed them to the Lord, who would fight for them.

The Rod: At the Lord's word he stretched out his hand, and the dead end became a way through the sea.

How does the same light mean protection to some and darkness to others?Which side of the pillar.

Followers: Those who follow the Lord find the light grows brighter under attack and keeps them from turning back.

Opposers: Those who attack God's people sink deeper into darkness and may end up as a sacrifice, like Pharaoh.
