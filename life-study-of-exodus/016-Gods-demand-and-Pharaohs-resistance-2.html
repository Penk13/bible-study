Exodus · Message 16

God's Demand and Pharaoh's Resistance (2)

Exodus is a book of pictures. In the second and third conflicts God first exposed the satanic source of Egyptian living, then turned its waters to blood: the life of the world issues in death.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Twelve Conflicts: The first was negotiation only; the second, a miracle that exposed but did not judge; then came the plagues.
  • The Rod a Serpent: What Egypt leaned on was, in God's eyes, the Devil. The exposure was mercy, not punishment.
  • Aaron's Rod Swallows: The magicians copied the sign, but their rods were swallowed (), as the gospel swallows the world's philosophies.
  • Water to Blood: Wherever the world's water is, river or vessel, it issues in death ().
  • A Hard Heart: Pharaoh's heart was stubborn from the start (); he went home unmoved ().

Ten Plagues in Four Groups

Behind every conflict was one demand: "Let My people go, that they may hold a feast to Me in the wilderness" (). Each plague was more severe than the last. The seven bowls of Revelation 16 will likewise bring God's people out of the world at the end of the age.

The Rod Exposed

Egypt relied on the riches of the Nile, but that "rod" was a serpent. Before God punishes the self or the flesh, He mercifully shows us the satanic nature of our fallen living.

The Magicians

Imitation

They turned rods to serpents and water to blood. Philosophers may also expose the vanity of worldly life, but they cannot remove the death.

Aaron's Rod

Swallowing

The gospel is not philosophy, yet it swallows every philosophy (). Preach without fear.

Blood by the River

God sent Moses to meet Pharaoh in the morning at the Nile, his place of ease. The fish died, the river stank, and digging for water found none: worldly pleasures end in blood.

Exposure or Judgment

Received, God's exposure is a merciful warning; rejected, it becomes judgment. So with the gospel: the word refused becomes the judge ().

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What is your "rod," the thing you lean on for living?The Nile's supply.

Egypt's Rod: The Egyptians trusted the natural resources of the Nile, not knowing they lived under Satan's usurping hand.

Today: Whatever sustains a life without God, however ordinary, is shown in God's light to be a serpent.

Why are the magicians' imitations no threat to the gospel?Bringing in versus taking away.

Similar Words: Philosophers may also say, "Do not love the world," and expose life's emptiness.

No Remedy: They can bring in the blood but cannot take it away. Only the gospel deals with death, and it swallows up their rods.

When has your "water" suddenly become blood?The first plague in experience.

Exposure: In the midst of a worldly pleasure, God may let it turn sour so that we see it issues in death.

Mercy: He did not kill the Egyptians; He warned them. Such moments are His merciful word to turn us.

What does Pharaoh's hard heart teach about God's dealings?Hard, not hardened.

Stubborn Nature: Pharaoh's heart was not soft and then hardened; it was stubborn from the beginning, and each conflict only exposed it.

Escalation: Refusing God's demand after exposure forced Him to deal more severely. Resistance does not stop God; it brings His next step.
