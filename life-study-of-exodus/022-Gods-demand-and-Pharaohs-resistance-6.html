Exodus · Message 22

God's Demand and Pharaoh's Resistance (6)

The twelfth conflict ended in the slaughter of the firstborn. Through twelve conflicts and ten plagues God displayed His sovereignty in Pharaoh and His mercy in Moses.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Made to Stand: God both hardened Pharaoh's heart and strengthened him to stand, so that His power and name would be declared ().
  • Both Useful: Moses and Pharaoh were a match; without either, God could not have displayed what He did.
  • Not a House Without Dead: At midnight the firstborn of Egypt died, and Pharaoh was subdued ().
  • Not a Dog Barked: Meanwhile Israel rested in peace, distinguished by the Lord ().
  • Work by Representing: Moses did not strive; as God's sent one he became great in Egypt ().

A Portrait of Sovereignty

Sovereignty is God's absolute right, authority, and power. Love is easy to know; sovereignty is not. These chapters exist to show it: one man demanding more each time, the other resisting harder, while the plagues dismantled every condition of Egyptian life.

Set Aside

Pharaoh

Hardened and upheld to stand through all twelve conflicts; God's sovereignty is seen in him.

Shown Mercy

Moses

Backed by the One in the heavens; God's mercy is seen in him ().

The Final Plague

The firstborn represented the strongest and most loved. God did not seek man's approval; the God who is love acted here according to sovereignty. Pharaoh released the people with their flocks, asked to be blessed, and let Egypt be plundered. Yet he was subdued only for a time; he pursued again until his army was buried in the Red Sea (14:4).

Under Sovereign Mercy

Knowing God's sovereignty makes us thankful for His mercy: a softened heart that repents quickly, a desire to meet with the saints, a new beginning each morning. We are destined to be sons of mercy, not Pharaohs.

The Way to Work for God

Moses neither fought nor labored. He came because he was sent and spoke what God told him, so Pharaoh was really dealing with God. An apostle is a sent one: we are the bush, the Lord is the fire, and the two are one ().

Striving

Praying mainly for our work and running in our own energy.

Representing

Staying in His presence until He saturates us, then going as His sent ones ().

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

In what sense was Pharaoh useful to God?Twelve conflicts.

A Stage: Had Pharaoh died early, God would have lost the occasion to show His power and declare His name.

Upheld: So God not only hardened him but strengthened him to stand to the end.

Why is the slaughter of the firstborn a matter of sovereignty, not love?Romans 9.

Two Attributes: God is love, but He is also sovereign. Romans 9 speaks of sovereignty, not love.

Two Peoples: The same night brought weeping in Egypt and quiet rest in Israel, where not even a dog barked.

How does recognizing sovereignty deepen our thanks for mercy?Not today's Pharaoh.

Contrast: Seeing what God did with Pharaoh shows how undeserved our soft heart is.

Daily: Our willingness to repent, confess, and seek Him is itself His sovereign mercy.

What would it mean for you to work as God's representative?The bush and the fire.

Contact First: Pray to touch the Lord and sense His heart more than to push the work.

Oneness: Go with Him and in Him, speaking His word, so that people deal with God, not with us.
