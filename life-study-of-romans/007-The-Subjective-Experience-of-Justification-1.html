Life-Study / Romans 007

Romans · Message 7

The Subjective Experience of Justification (1)

Romans 4 goes deeper than chapter 3. Justification is God calling us back to Himself through a chain of reactions: Christ transfused into us as faith, faith reckoned as righteousness, Isaac given and offered back. Its goal is not only our salvation but God's purpose: a seed that becomes a kingdom, the Body.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Fall and Justification: The fall is being kept from God by anything that is not God, good or bad. Justification calls us out of everything back to Him.
  • Faith Is an Echo: In the gospel of glory Christ transfuses Himself into us (). Our believing is the echo of that sound, and God reckons it as righteousness ().
  • Positional to Dispositional: Christ's death gives positional justification; the risen Christ in the heavens proves it () and lives in us a dispositional one.
  • For God's Purpose: Genesis 15 never mentions sin. Abraham was justified over a seed as the stars (), heirs of the world ().
  • Ishmael, Isaac, the Altar: Self-effort produces Ishmael (). God gives Isaac, asks for him on the altar, and returns a nation. Our individual Christ offered back yields the Body ().

Reactions Between God and Man

Justification is not a transaction but a series of reactions, each one bringing more of Christ:

Justification for God's Purpose

As a young believer, Witness Lee thought Romans 4 was unnecessary: chapter 3 seemed to finish justification. But redemption is a process, not God's purpose. Chapter 4 shows the purpose.

Romans 3

Justified from Sins

We are sinful; Christ's blood redeems us; God can righteously justify us. Correct, yet centered on our salvation.

Romans 4 · Genesis 15

Justified for a Kingdom

No sin is mentioned; the issue is a seed () to inherit the world () and exercise God's dominion as the corporate man of Genesis 1 ().

Chosen for His Purpose

God did not call you primarily for heaven; He called you for His purpose. If you are involved with His purpose, your salvation is secure. If you care only for your salvation, you may miss His purpose.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

If faith is an "echo," where does it come from, and why can't we produce it?Read 2 Corinthians 4:4 and recall the day you first believed.

The Sound First: In the gospel of glory, Christ transfused Himself into us (). Our appreciation and believing were a reaction to Him.

Impossible Not to Believe: Because faith is Christ believing in us, no argument afterward could take it out of us. That faith returns to God, and He reckons it as righteousness ().

Why does Paul connect justification to inheriting the world in Romans 4:13?Genesis 15 never mentions sin. What was it about?

A Seed for a Kingdom: Abraham's faith was reckoned over God's word about a seed like the stars (). God wanted a corporate people to express Him and exercise His dominion, as He intended in Genesis 1.

Beyond Salvation: Justification enables Abraham's heirs to inherit the world () for God's kingdom. Salvation is not an end in itself.

What is our "Ishmael," and why must it be cast out?Galatians 4:24 says Hagar typifies the law.

Good Works of the Self: Right after believing we resolve to behave well, love our spouse, and submit. All that self-effort produces is Ishmael, the works of the law ().

Circumcision: God says, "Stop your doing." We must be terminated so that Christ, the real Isaac, can be born in us as the living righteousness of God.

What does it mean to offer our "Isaac" on the altar today?Consider the believer who says, "My experience of Christ is enough; I don't need the church."

The Hardest Offering: Once we enjoy Christ personally, we guard Him as a treasure. When God asks us to bring that Christ into the church life, we resist.

Thousands Return: Abraham offered one Isaac and received a nation (). Offering our individual Christ yields the Body, many members in one (), the kingdom of righteousness, peace, and joy ().
