Romans · Message 16

The Freedom of the Spirit in Our Spirit (3)

Romans 7 has the indwelling sin; Romans 8 has the indwelling Christ. The Spirit of God, the Spirit of Christ, and Christ are one, indwelling us as life with fourfold riches. The flesh is hopeless, but as we set our mind on the spirit, the Spirit puts to death the practices of the body and gives life to our whole being.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Two Indwellers: Sin dwells in us, the factor of wretchedness (). Christ dwells in us, the factor of blessedness ().
  • Three Names, One Person: "The Spirit of God," "the Spirit of Christ," and "Christ" are used interchangeably (–). Christ must be the Spirit to indwell us.
  • Fourfold Life: Life in the Spirit (), in our spirit (), in our mind (), and in our mortal body ().
  • The Flesh Is Hopeless: It is enmity against God, not subject to His law, cannot be, and cannot please Him (–). God judged it at the flood ().
  • Our Cooperation: We are not debtors to the flesh (). Set the mind on the spirit, and by the Spirit put to death the practices of the body ().

Romans 7 and Romans 8

Romans 8 is a sharp contrast to Romans 7. Something other than sin now lodges within us.

The Indwelling of Christ the Spirit

We are in the spirit, not in the flesh, "if indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you" (). "Dwells" means makes home. He may be in us as a guest without yet settling in.

The Spirit of God

The condition for being in the spirit: He must make His home in us, with adequate room.

The Spirit of Christ

Do not be discouraged. Having the Spirit of Christ, we are of Christ, even if He has not yet taken full possession.

Christ Himself

In chapter 6 we are in Christ; in chapter 8 Christ is in us. Abiding in Him brings His abiding in us ().

Sin Not Eradicated

Though Christ is in us, the body is still dead because of sin. A preacher who taught that sin was uprooted shared park tickets to sneak in his disciples and called it "a little weakness." Sin is sin. What the cross accomplished is experienced only by staying in the all-inclusive Spirit, moment by moment, like breathing.

Life Imparted, Through Our Cooperation

Our spirit is life because of righteousness: as the prodigal needed the best robe before the feast (), Christ is first our righteousness, then our life. The Spirit of resurrection then gives life even to our mortal bodies (), like electricity installed in a building whose flow we must not frustrate.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

How do Romans 8:9–11 show that Christ is the indwelling Spirit?Trace the three terms Paul uses interchangeably.

Synonyms: "The Spirit of God" becomes "the Spirit of Christ," then "Christ" (–), then the indwelling Spirit (). The resurrected Christ is the life-giving Spirit ().

Why It Matters: If Christ were not the Spirit, He could never indwell us and impart Himself as life.

What is the difference between having the Spirit and the Spirit dwelling in us?A guest versus one who makes his home.

Already of Christ: Every believer has the Spirit of Christ and is of Christ ().

Making Home: Whether He settles in us with room is conditional. Only as He takes possession of our inner being are we practically in the spirit.

Why can we never place hope in the flesh, even after being saved?Count the four points of Romans 8:7–8.

Final Verdict: The flesh is enmity against God, is not subject to His law, cannot be, and cannot please Him (–). Saved or not, flesh is flesh.

Not Debtors: A condemned house must not be lived in. We owe the flesh nothing ().

How do we practically put to death the practices of the body?Where is your mind set?

The Mind's Direction: Romans 8 gives no rules. Turn the mind from outward things to the spirit; restlessness signals death, rest signals life.

By the Spirit: With the mind on the spirit, the Spirit applies Christ's death to the practices of the body (), daily, and our walk spontaneously fulfills the law ().
