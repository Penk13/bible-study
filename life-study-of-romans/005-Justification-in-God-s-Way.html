Romans · Message 5

Justification in God's Way

Justification is God approving us by the standard of His own righteousness, which is God Himself embodied in Christ. It rests on Christ's redemption and takes place upon Christ as the propitiation cover, where the law is silenced, God's glory is satisfied, and God and man approve each other.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Righteousness Is a Person: God's righteousness is God Himself; Christ is made our righteousness (); and in Him we are made the righteousness of God ().
  • Justification Defined: God's action of approving us according to His standard, not ours. Our righteousness is a quarter inch high; His is unlimited. Redemption is the basis.
  • Three Terms: Propitiation deals with sins between two parties; redemption buys back what was lost; reconciliation deals with sins and enmity ().
  • On the Propitiation Cover: God set forth Christ as the place of propitiation (). The law is under it, the cherubim of glory over it, and the blood satisfies both ().
  • By the Faith of Jesus: We have no faith of our own; we believe by His faith (; ). Boasting is excluded, and one God justifies Jews and Gentiles alike ().

The Righteousness of God

Whatever God is in His justice and rightness is His righteousness, and that is God Himself. It is a Person, not merely an attribute.

Propitiation, Redemption, Reconciliation

Justification relates to three other terms. Each solves a different problem between God and man:

TermProblemMeaningVerses
PropitiationSins: an offense and a debtAppeasing the offended party by satisfying his demands; at-one-ment; ;
RedemptionLost: we belonged to God but were lostRepossessing at a cost, meeting the demands of righteousness, holiness, and glory
ReconciliationEnmity as well as sinsIncludes propitiation; the issue of redemption with justification

The New Testament uses three forms of one root: hilasmos, the propitiatory sacrifice (); hilastērion, the place of propitiation (; ); and hilaskomai, the act of propitiating (). The tax collector prayed, in effect, "God, propitiate for me" ().

Christ, the Propitiation Cover

Paul had the ark in mind. Under the cover lay the law, the testimony exposing what God is. Above it were the cherubim of glory. On the day of expiation the blood was sprinkled on the cover (), and there God met with man ().

📦

The Expiatory Cover

Law beneath condemning, cherubim above observing. Sprinkled blood satisfied the righteous law and God's glory, so God could speak with man lawfully.

✝️

Christ Set Forth

God set forth Christ as the propitiation place (). Standing on Him, the law is under our feet and God's glory rejoices over our head.

Mutual Approval

On this cover we justify God and God justifies us (). In experience, we approved God first: He patiently convinced us He was right, and then He approved us.

Righteousness Manifested and Shown

God's righteousness has been manifested apart from law, through the faith of Jesus Christ (). It has been shown in two ways:

Old Testament Saints ·

Passing By Sins

Their sins were covered, not removed. The blood was a promissory note, redeemed when Christ died (). Righteousness required God to pass by them.

New Testament Saints ·

Justifying Us

Freely by His grace through redemption (). Since Christ paid in full, God, to be just, must justify the one who believes.

So boasting is excluded (). And one God justifies the circumcision out of faith, since they already stand as His people, and the uncircumcision through faith, since they come from afar (). One God, one way, preparing for one Body.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why should we say "Christ is my righteousness" rather than "I have the righteousness of Christ"?Compare 1 Corinthians 1:30 with 2 Corinthians 5:21.

A Person, Not an Attribute: God's righteousness is God Himself, and Christ is God embodied. So it is Christ Himself, not a quality of His, that God made our righteousness ().

Becoming Righteousness: Because Christ is wrought into us and we are put into Him, we are not merely made righteous but made the righteousness of God (), as water blended with tea becomes tea.

How do propitiation, redemption, and reconciliation differ, and why do we need all three?Ask what problem each one solves.

Three Problems: As sinners we owed God and offended Him; we needed propitiation. As His lost possession we needed buying back; that is redemption. As His enemies we needed reconciliation, which includes propitiation ().

Justification's Place: Redemption is the basis of justification, and reconciliation is the issue of redemption with justification. Each term describes one aspect of the one work of Christ.

What does it mean that Christ is the propitiation cover, and why does Paul mention God's glory in Romans 3:23?Picture the ark: law beneath, cherubim above, blood on the lid.

Two Requirements: We broke the law and fell short of glory (). The law under the cover and the cherubim of glory above it () picture both demands.

A Place to Meet: Propitiation is not only an act but a place. Upon Christ the law is covered and silenced, glory is satisfied, and God can meet and speak with us without contradicting Himself ().

If we have no faith of our own, how do we believe?Consider "the faith of Jesus Christ" in Romans 3:22 and Galatians 2:20.

His Faith in Us: Faith cannot be generated by us; Jesus is its Author and Finisher (). We live by the faith of the Son of God ().

Practice: The more we examine ourselves, the faster faith disappears. Forget yourself and say, "Lord Jesus, I love You." Faith rises at once, because it is Jesus believing within us.
