Romans · Message 22

God's Selection, Our Destiny (1)

Our eternal destiny rests on God's selection: of Him who calls, of His mercy, of His sovereignty, and by the righteousness of faith. Its goal is that we be vessels of mercy to contain God and express His glory.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Parenthesis and Bridge: Romans 9–11 is a parenthesis in the life process, yet it also links chapter 8 to chapter 12.
  • Of Him Who Calls: Before the twins were born or had done good or bad, God chose Jacob, not by works or by birth ().
  • Of His Mercy: Not of him who wills or runs (). Mercy reaches further than grace, into a condition unworthy of grace.
  • Of His Sovereignty: The potter has authority over the clay (). We were made vessels to contain God.
  • By Faith: The nations gained righteousness out of faith; Israel, zealous for its own righteousness, stumbled at Christ ().

Paul's Burden and Christ's Glory

Paul's conscience bore witness "in the Holy Spirit," showing the conscience is part of our spirit. His grief for Israel was so intense that he prayed to be accursed from Christ for them (). Such a prayer came from his own love, not from the Lord; God answered, but not in Paul's way. Then, listing Israel's privileges, Paul bursts out that Christ is over all, God blessed forever (; ).

Of Him Who Calls

Not all who come out of Israel are Israel. Ishmael and Isaac were both of Abraham, yet only the children of promise are counted as the seed (); the natural birth is not enough. Even of Isaac's twin sons only one was chosen: "Jacob I have loved, but Esau I have hated" ().

Grace

A Gift to a Friend

Given where the receiver's condition matches the giver's.

Mercy

A Gift to a Beggar

Reaches across the gap to the pitiful and unworthy, as with crafty Jacob ().

Mercy brought us into grace, and even now we first obtain mercy, then find grace (). Knowing this, we neither trust our effort nor despair at our failure. Even Pharaoh served God's purpose by showing forth His power.

Vessels to Contain God

We are clay; God is the potter. Who are we to answer back? Romans 9:21 reveals why God created man: to be His vessel, with God as the content. We hold this treasure in earthen vessels (), prepared beforehand for glory ().

The Goal of Selection

Not merely to be saved, nor even to serve as servants, priests, and kings, but to contain God as life and express Him, one with Him in life and nature, consummating in the New Jerusalem.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why did God choose Jacob rather than Esau?Before they had done good or bad.

Not by Works: The choice was made before birth, that God's purpose by selection might stand, of Him who calls ().

Not by Birth: Both were sons of Isaac. God's selection depends on nothing but Himself.

How is mercy different from grace, and why does it matter to us?A friend and a beggar.

The Difference: Grace meets a fitting condition; mercy reaches a poor, unworthy one. God said "mercy," not "grace," in .

For Us: It is not of the one who wills or runs (). Our hope rests on His mercy, so we are freed from both self-trust and despair.

What does the potter and the clay reveal about why we exist?Container and content.

Sovereignty: As creatures we have no ground to argue with the Creator ().

Purpose: We were made vessels of mercy to contain God and make known the riches of His glory (). This is our destiny.

How can zeal for God cause us to miss God's way?Israel's own righteousness.

The Danger: Israel had zeal without full knowledge, seeking to establish its own righteousness and not submitting to God's ().

The Way: Righteousness is out of faith, and that righteousness is Christ. Any effort to please God by keeping law or doing good misses His salvation.
