Romans · Message 14

The Freedom of the Spirit in Our Spirit (1)

Romans 7 is bondage, law, and flesh; Romans 8 is freedom, Spirit, and spirit. The way to live the divine life is the Spirit of life. The processed God, now the Spirit, dwells in our spirit with a law that supplies rather than demands, spreading life from our spirit to our mind and even to our mortal body.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Romans 7 vs. 8: Bondage vs. freedom; law vs. Spirit; flesh vs. spirit. "No condemnation" ().
  • Life Needs the Spirit: Romans 1–6 mention life nine times but never say how. Romans 8:2 joins them: "the Spirit of life." "It is the Spirit who gives life" ().
  • Fourfold Life: The Spirit is life (), our spirit is life (), the mind set on the spirit is life (), and our mortal body is given life ().
  • The Processed God: Through incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection, and ascension, the "raw" God became the Spirit of life, like broth easy to take in. His law supplies; God's law only demands ().
  • A Miniature Eden: Self in the soul, Satan in the body, God in the spirit: three persons, three lives, three laws. Our spirit is the dining table where we feast on Christ as the life-giving Spirit ().

The Fourfold Life

Life is the goal of God's salvation: saved in life (), reigning in life (), walking in newness of life (). Romans 8 shows how: the Spirit spreading life through our whole being.

NKJV renders 8:5–6 "according to the Spirit" and "spiritually minded"; Witness Lee reads the first "spirit" in verse 5 as our human spirit, and the mind "set on the spirit."

Four Laws in One Person

We are complicated because we passed through creation, the fall, and salvation. Each gave us a life, and each life has its law.

The Processed God at Our Table

Romans reveals God progressively: in creation, redemption, justification, reconciliation, identification, and now in our spirit.

🧊

The Refrigerator

Raw groceries: God as Jehovah, the Almighty, with a law that only demands. Not yet suitable for eating.

🍲

The Dining Table

Romans 8: the processed God as the Spirit of life, easy to take in like broth. Christ became the life-giving Spirit (; ), with us in our spirit ().

The Robe and the Fatted Calf

The returning son received the best robe: Christ as righteousness, satisfying the father. But the son was still hungry, so the fatted calf was killed: Christ processed to be our food (). The letter kills; the Spirit gives life ().

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

How does Romans 8:2 answer the question left by Romans 1–6?Those chapters say to live, reign, and walk in life. How?

Life Without a Way: Romans 1–6 speak of having life, being saved in life, reigning in life, and walking in newness of life, but not how.

The Spirit Is the Way: Romans 8:2 joins the Spirit with life. We cannot separate them: "It is the Spirit who gives life" (). Walking by the Spirit is walking in life.

What is the difference between the law of God and the law of the Spirit of life?Compare a demand with a bowl of broth.

Demand: God's law is holy and good, and the holier it is, the more it demands. It cannot give life (). Israel broke it before Moses even came down.

Supply: The law of the Spirit of life is the spontaneous power of the processed God within our spirit. It supplies, and so it frees us from the law of sin and death ().

How can our mind and even our body become "life"?Trace Romans 8:6, 10, and 11.

From the Center Out: The Spirit first makes our spirit life (). When we set our mind on the spirit, the mind becomes life and peace ().

Even the Body: The indwelling Spirit gives life to our mortal bodies (). The body of sin and death becomes a body of life.

Why does Witness Lee call our spirit "the dining table"?Think of the prodigal son: robe and calf.

Beyond the Robe: Christ as our righteousness satisfies God, but we remain hungry. The fatted calf, Christ processed on the cross, is for our eating ().

Where We Eat: Christ in the heavens would be too far. As the life-giving Spirit He is in our spirit (), and there we feast on everything He is.
