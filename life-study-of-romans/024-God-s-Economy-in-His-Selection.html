Romans · Message 24

God's Economy in His Selection

In His selection God has an economy, a divine arrangement over the whole human race: a remnant kept by grace, the nations saved through Israel's stumbling, and Israel restored through the nations' mercy, all ending in praise.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • A Remnant by Grace: As in Elijah's day, God still keeps a remnant, chosen by grace, not by works ().
  • Stumbled, Not Fallen: Israel's false step brought salvation to the nations (). They are set aside for a time, not cast away.
  • Dough and Root: The patriarchs are the firstfruit dough and the root of the olive tree; the dough satisfies God, the root's fatness satisfies us.
  • Mercy Through Disobedience: God shut up all in disobedience to show mercy to all ().
  • Out of, Through, To Him: Paul ends in praise: all things are of Him, through Him, and to Him ().

A Remnant Reserved by Grace

After chapter 10 we might think God had given Israel up. Paul answers, "Certainly not!" and points to himself, an Israelite (). Elijah thought he alone was left, yet God had kept seven thousand who had not bowed to Baal. The principle still holds: however degraded Christianity becomes, God reserves a remnant for His purpose. We have no ground to boast, since if it is by grace it is not by works ().

The rest were hardened, with eyes that do not see and ears that do not hear. The same blindness and bent backs can be seen among many religious people today.

The Nations Grafted In

Israel stumbled but did not fall. Their false step became the riches of the nations. They are cast aside for a time, not cast away. The patriarchs Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob are the holy firstfruit and the holy root, so the lump and branches are holy too ().

Romans 9

Clay

Lifeless, yet made into vessels to contain Christ for God's glory.

Romans 11

Dough

Fine flour with life, offered to God as food for His satisfaction.

Romans 11

Root of Fatness

The olive tree's rich root, which we as grafted branches share and enjoy ().

Grafting is a matter of life. The nations need not merely change religion but receive the life of the root, which is Christ. Yet they stand by faith and must not be high-minded; the natural branches were broken off through unbelief.

Israel Restored, God Praised

Israel's hardening lasts until the fullness of the nations, the full number of Gentile converts, has come in (). This differs from "the times of the Gentiles," which concerns the end of Gentile power. Then all Israel will be saved (), for God's gifts and calling are irrevocable ().

Paul then bursts into praise for the depth of God's riches, wisdom, and knowledge (). All things came out of Him in the past, exist through Him now, and will be to Him in the future.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What encouragement does the remnant give us when the situation looks hopeless?Elijah's seven thousand.

Not Alone: Elijah felt he alone remained, but God had reserved seven thousand. Even now there is a remnant according to the selection of grace ().

No Boasting: Being kept is wholly of grace, not of our work (). We should neither despair nor be proud.

What is the difference between being cast away and being cast aside?Stumbled, not fallen.

Cast Away: To be given up for good. Paul firmly denies that God has done this to His people ().

Cast Aside: To be put aside for a period. Israel stumbled but did not fall, and will be grafted in again, since God's calling is irrevocable ().

Why is grafting into the olive tree more than changing religion?A matter of life.

Definition: A grafted branch receives the life of the cultivated tree. We partake of the root and its fatness (), and that life is Christ.

Deviation: Many have moved from a heathen religion to the Christian religion without ever receiving Christ's life. Outward change is not grafting.

How can even disobedience serve God's economy?An enclosure for mercy.

Opportunity: Man's disobedience gives God's mercy room to act, and mercy brings salvation. God shut up all in disobedience to have mercy on all ().

Response: We can only praise. All things are out of Him, through Him, and to Him ().
