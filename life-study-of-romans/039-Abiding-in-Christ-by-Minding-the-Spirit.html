Romans · Message 39

Abiding in Christ by Minding the Spirit

John 15 tells us to abide in Christ; Romans 8 shows how. Christ as the life-giving Spirit is in our spirit, and setting our mind on the mingled spirit is the abiding.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • A Progression: John 15 gives the command to abide; Romans 8 gives the practical way.
  • Where Is Christ? Both at God's right hand and in us, in our spirit (; ).
  • What Is the "Me"? The Son, who embodies the Father, realized as the life-giving Spirit.
  • How Do We Abide? By setting our mind on the mingled spirit; the minding is the abiding, and its issue is life and peace.
  • Two Indwellers: Sin makes its home in our flesh, Christ as the Spirit in our spirit; our mind stands between them.

Three Questions

"Abide in Me" sounds simple but is hard to practice. Following Christ is easy to picture; abiding in someone who is in the third heaven is not. Like a long letter read to the end, the Bible's revelation is progressive, and Romans 8 answers what John 15 leaves open.

The "Me" We Abide In

The Triune God is not given for analysis but for dispensing Himself into us. Whenever we call on the name of the Lord Jesus, we receive the Spirit, because the Spirit is the reality of that name. All we need, from patience to victory, is this living One in our spirit.

Two Indwellers and the Mind

The believer is a miniature Eden: the tree of life in the spirit, the tree of knowledge in the flesh. The word "dwells" in Romans 7 has the root "house": sin, the embodiment of Satan, makes its home in our flesh (). No doctrine or method can subdue it; only the Lord can. Our mind, our very self, decides which one we turn to.

Romans 7

Sin in the Flesh

The mind resolving to do good on its own only activates the law of sin and is taken captive ().

Romans 8

Christ in the Spirit

The mind set on the spirit touches a living Person; sin is subdued and the result is life and peace ().

Letting Him Make His Home

If Christ is in us, our spirit is life, though the body is still dead (). But if He is allowed to dwell, to settle in every part, He gives life even to our mortal bodies (). A guest kept to one corner of a house cannot be at home.

Praying to Stay in the Spirit

Pray not mainly for your needs, or even for love and patience, but to keep in touch with the Lord. Praise Him as your patience, and patience will flow out spontaneously.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is "abide through the Holy Spirit" an incomplete answer?One Person, not two.

Not Another Person: The Spirit is not a go-between; He is the reality of the Son Himself ().

So: Contacting the Spirit in our spirit is contacting Christ, and remaining there is abiding in Him.

What is the difference between setting the mind on the spirit and making up the mind to do good?Romans 7 vs. Romans 8.

Independent Effort: Making up the mind to do good stirs up indwelling sin and ends in defeat ().

Dependence: Setting the mind on the spirit turns us to the One who has already defeated Satan; apart from Him we can do nothing ().

Christ is in me. Why can my body still feel dead?Is versus dwells.

The Beginning: Christ being in us makes our spirit life, unconditionally.

The Continuation: Life reaches the body only as He is given freedom to make His home in all our inward parts ().

How can prayer distract me from the spirit?Praise, not petition.

Occupied with Needs: Praying over our lack of love or patience keeps us focused on ourselves.

Touching the Person: Calling "Lord Jesus, I love You" keeps the mind on the spirit, and Christ is lived out spontaneously.
