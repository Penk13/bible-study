Life-Study / Romans 006

Romans · Message 6

The Example of Justification

Romans is written from experience, so with the doctrine of justification Paul gives a living example: Abraham. Called out of the created race step by step, he became a believing one, trusting the God who calls things not being as being and gives life to the dead. His faith was reckoned as righteousness, and the risen Christ is the proof.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Created vs. Called: Adam heads the created race, which ended in idolatry at Babel and fornication in Sodom, the history behind Romans 1. Abraham heads the called race.
  • Called by Glory: The God of glory appeared to Abraham (). He was not absolute, yet God was. Step by step he left country, kindred, father's house (), Lot, and finally himself.
  • The Believing One: Not knowing where he was going, he could only trust. He believed God who calls things not being as being and gives life to the dead ().
  • Reckoned as Righteousness: Faith is Christ believing in us, and God reckons it as righteousness (). Circumcision came afterward, only as a seal ().
  • Father and Heirs: Abraham is father of all who believe (; ), heirs of the world (). The risen Christ proves our justification ().

The Called One

Genesis has two sections: the created race under Adam (chapters 1 to mid-11), and the called race under Abraham. When man gave God up, God gave man up, and then called one complete person, a man with his wife, out of the hopeless race.

The Believing One

Believing means terminating ourselves: we are nothing, have nothing, can do nothing. The God Abraham trusted is described two ways ():

Out of Nothing

Calls Things Not Being as Being

When there was no possibility of an heir, God said, "There shall be an Isaac," and Isaac was born. He is the Creator.

Out of Death ·

Gives Life to the Dead

Offering Isaac, Abraham reckoned that God could raise him from the dead and received him back in a figure.

Apply It to Your Situation

Is your church poor? Your God calls things not being as being. Is your locality dead? He gives life to the dead. Witness Lee arrived in Taiwan in 1949 discouraged; within five years the saints grew from 350 to 20,000. Do not say "my spouse is poor"; say "my God is not poor."

Reckoned, Sealed, and Proved

What follows from Abraham's faith shows how justification works for every believer:

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What is the difference between being created and being called, and which race do we belong to?Trace Genesis from Babel to Abraham, and compare Romans 1.

Two Races: The created race under Adam gave God up and ended in idolatry and fornication, the very history Romans 1 describes. God gave that race up, then called Abraham out of it to head a new race.

Our Standing: Believers belong to the called race. We were called by the same God of glory () to come out of everything that is not God Himself.

Why was Abraham's last and hardest departure leaving himself?Consider Hagar and Ishmael, and Abraham at a hundred years old.

Still in Himself: Having left country, kindred, father's house, and Lot, Abraham still acted on Sarah's well-meant plan and produced Ishmael. He had forsaken many things but not himself.

As Good as Dead: Only when his body was as good as dead () did he stop doing and let God do everything. That is when he became a fully called person.

How do we apply faith in "the God who gives life to the dead" to a discouraging church or family situation?Read Romans 4:17 and the Taiwan testimony.

The Right Place for God: A poor or dead situation is exactly where the God who creates from nothing and raises the dead can work (). He does not give life to the living.

Watch Your Words: Complaining makes things worse. Declare instead, "My situation is poor, but my God is not." Then call on Him and believe.

Why is the resurrected Christ the proof of our justification?Read Romans 4:25: "raised because of our justification."

God Satisfied: Christ's death met every righteous requirement (). If God had not accepted it, Christ would not have been raised. His seat at God's right hand is the receipt.

Accepted in Him: More than proof, the risen Christ lives in us a life that God can justify and always accepts ().
