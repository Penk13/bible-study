Romans · Message 23

God's Selection, Our Destiny (2)

God's selection is carried out through Christ. Romans 9 shows that we are vessels; Romans 10 shows how the empty vessels are filled: by believing in the near Christ with our heart and calling on Him with our mouth.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • The End of the Law: By fulfilling the law Christ terminated it, so God's righteousness is now given to all who believe ().
  • Processed Christ: No one needs to bring Christ down or up; He has already come down in incarnation and come up from the abyss in resurrection.
  • Near You: As the life-giving Spirit and the living Word, He is in our mouth and in our heart ().
  • Heart and Mouth: Believing with the heart results in righteousness; confessing, calling, with the mouth results in salvation ().
  • Rich to All Who Call: Calling on the Lord is not only for being saved but for enjoying His riches ().

Christ, the End of the Law

The Jews held on to the law to build up their own righteousness, not seeing that Christ had completed and ended it on the cross. Many believers repeat this in principle: after being saved they make rules for themselves and strive to keep them to please God. Christ is the end of every such regulation. He Himself is the living righteousness for all who believe.

Paul's word on ascending to heaven and descending into the abyss quietly includes incarnation and resurrection. The abyss is the region of death and Satan's dark power, which Christ entered after death, conquered, and left in resurrection. Having passed through this whole process, He became the life-giving Spirit (), available to anyone, anywhere.

The Way to Fill the Vessels

Romans 9 calls us vessels of mercy prepared for glory (), but a vessel by itself is empty. Romans 10 supplies the way to be filled. Unlike a hammer or an axe, a vessel has an opening; our mouth is that opening, made for calling on the Lord. "Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it" ().

Romans 9

The Vessels

Selected and made to contain God, yet empty in themselves.

Romans 10

The Filling

Believe with the heart, call with the mouth, and the rich Lord fills us ().

"In your mouth and in your heart" implies that the resurrected Christ is like air, the breath we take in. We exercise the mouth to breathe Him in, the heart to receive Him, and the spirit to retain Him.

Calling on the Name of the Lord

Calling differs from silent prayer: it is to invoke the Lord by name, audibly, even to cry out. It is a spiritual breathing, exhaling our burdens and inhaling the Lord as our strength and rest. When we say "Lord Jesus," we are in the Spirit (); we get the person by calling the name, and so we drink of Him as well.

For others to call, they must believe; to believe, they must hear; to hear, someone must be sent to preach (). Christ is preached and heard, received by the nations and rejected by Israel.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What does it mean that Christ is the end of the law, and how can believers miss it?Self-made regulations.

Definition: Christ fulfilled the law and so terminated it; righteousness now comes by believing in Him ().

Deviation: Like the Jews, saved ones can set up personal rules to please God. Christ ended all such rules so that He may be our living righteousness.

Why does Paul say the word is in our mouth and in our heart?Like air.

Nearness: Having passed through incarnation and resurrection, Christ is the life-giving Spirit and the living Word, nearer than anything ().

Breath: Only air can be in both mouth and heart. We breathe Him in by calling and receive Him by believing.

How are the vessels of Romans 9 filled?A vessel has a mouth.

The Opening: We were made with a mouth so we could be filled. As we open it wide and call, the Lord fills it ().

The Supply: The same Lord is rich to all who call upon Him (). Romans 9 gives the vessels; Romans 10 gives the way to fill them.

How is calling on the Lord different from praying silently, and what does it do in us?Breathing and drinking.

Audible: To call is to invoke Him by name, even to cry out, as the saints did from Enosh on ().

Experience: We breathe out pressure and heaviness and breathe in the Lord. Saying "Lord Jesus" puts us in the Spirit (), so we drink of Him day by day.
