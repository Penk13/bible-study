Romans · Message 30

The Consummation of the Gospel

The long conclusion of Romans holds no doctrine. Hidden under its list of greetings is the ultimate consummation of God's gospel: the practical church life.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Treasures Under the Trees: The greetings are the trees of a forest; beneath them lie the churches, mentioned five times in Romans 16.
  • The Offering of the Nations: Paul served Christ to the Gentiles as a priest and offered them, sanctified, to God ().
  • Communication in Love: He went to the Gentiles with Christ, returned to Judea with their gifts, and came to Rome in the fullness of Christ's blessing.
  • Satan Crushed, Grace Dispensed: Both happen under the feet of the saints in the church life ().
  • Established: Our remaining need is to be established by the gospel, the preaching of Christ, and the revelation of the mystery ().

Ministering Christ, Offering the Nations

Paul was like a waiter at a universal table, serving Christ as food until Christ became the transforming element in the Gentiles. Then, as a priest, he presented them to God as a sweet offering, sanctified in the Holy Spirit. Our gospel must be uplifted in the same way: not merely a heaven-going message, but ministering Christ to hungry sinners. Out of this came a practical love: former Gentile "swine" gave their possessions to their Jewish brothers. Spirituality that never touches material things is impractical.

The Fellowship of Concern

Paul's greetings show his far-reaching care for the saints within the churches and between them. Each mention of the church reveals a mark of the proper church life ().

Romans 1 to Romans 16

Chapter one shows vile, condemned sinners; chapter sixteen shows holy, glorious, practical churches. Between them lies God's long process of redemption, justification, sanctification, glorification, selection, and transformation.

The Concluding Praise

The mystery hidden through the ages has two sides: Christ in the believers as their life (), and the church as His Body expressing His fullness (). Romans shows believers baptized into Christ, with Christ wrought into them, built into one Body, and expressed as local churches. Romans 15 and 16 name a rich God: of endurance and encouragement, of hope, of peace, eternal, and only wise. His gospel consummates in the practical church life.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is the conclusion of Romans so long and so practical?The forest and its treasures.

The Goal: After transformation, Paul still had to show the consummation of the gospel, the church life, and he showed it in practice rather than doctrine.

Hidden Treasure: The greetings seem like a list of hard names, but under them are the churches and the virtues that make church life real.

How does ministering Christ differ from a heaven-going gospel?A waiter and a priest.

Content: We serve Christ as food so that He becomes the sanctifying element within people.

Result: They become an offering acceptable to God, and their transformed hearts show love in practical ways ().

Why is Satan crushed under the feet of the church people?Alone, we are prey.

Corporate Victory: Individually it is hard to fight Satan; one with the Body, he is under our feet and we enjoy the God of peace.

Grace: The church is also where the Lord dispenses His grace to all the saints ().

Which of the five aspects of church life is weakest in me today?Serve, risk, open, own, host.

Practice: Serving at cost, caring for all the churches, opening our home, calling it Christ's church, and hospitality are not theories but daily choices.

Blessing: The more we open ourselves and our homes, the richer the church life and the fuller our experience of Christ.
