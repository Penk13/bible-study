Romans · Message 20

Heirs of Glory (3)

God predestinated us to be conformed to the image of His firstborn Son. Glorification is not a sudden event but the issue of growth and maturity, and the molding that brings us there requires suffering.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Conformed for Glory: Christ, the firstborn Son, is the prototype; we, His many brothers, are being conformed to His image (; ).
  • Glory Is the Issue of Maturity: To expect rapture without growth is to dream. A harvest comes only when the crop is ripe ().
  • Suffering for Growth: Suffering not only intensifies glory; it also speeds our growth, as the scorching sun ripens the crop ().
  • Our Predestiny: Not a happy place, but conformity to the Son, that He may be the firstborn among many brothers for God's kingdom and Christ's Body.
  • Why Things Happen: The Spirit groans in our groaning with a definite purpose: that we be molded into the image of the Son ().

From Sprout to Blossom

A carnation seed sprouts, grows, and only then blossoms. No sprout blossoms overnight; that is against the law of life. Many Christians chase prophetic dates and signs, but every prediction has failed. The rapture is a harvest, and it awaits ripeness: we must grow until we arrive at a full-grown man ().

The Mold and the Oven

God has put us into Christ as dough into a mold. Being kneaded under pressure is not enough; the dough must also be baked so the shape is fixed. God arranges our environment, family, and even our face. To our feeling much of it is suffering, but it is His sovereign provision for growth, and the present sufferings cannot compare with the coming glory ().

Sanctification

Saturated

A change in nature and disposition by God's holy nature. No pattern, and it may not require much suffering.

Conformation

Molded

Pressed into the pattern of the firstborn Son. There is a mold, and without suffering we cannot take its shape.

The Groaning Spirit

Whatever we do in Romans 8, the Spirit does too: we cry "Abba," He cries; we groan, He groans. We groan with little understanding, but He groans with a purpose too heavenly to utter, interceding according to God (). The Father then answers by causing all things to work together for good ().

"Lord, Do Your Best"

The elders prayed this for a dear brother with a peculiar disposition. He was ill twice and asked why; they knew, but could only pray in his manner. In time he was delivered from his disposition, then healed, and could say, "Now I know."

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is it a dream to expect rapture without growth in life?A harvest and a ripe crop.

The Principle: Rapture is transfiguration and glorification, and glorification is the issue of maturity. The harvest comes only when the grain ripens ().

The Lesson: Rather than fixing dates or naming the antichrist, we should look at the crop. Our part is to grow.

What is our destiny according to Romans 8:29?Not a place, but an image.

The Image: God predestinated us to be conformed to the image of His Son (), decreed before creation.

The Goal: That the only begotten becomes the firstborn among many brothers: many sons for God's kingdom and many brothers for Christ's Body, a corporate expression of God.

How is conformation different from sanctification?The mold.

Sanctification: Being saturated with God's holy nature; a change in disposition, with no pattern.

Conformation: Being shaped to a definite pattern, Christ Himself. This needs pressure and fire, so it needs suffering.

What do you say when an unpleasant situation comes to you?A child crying at a good parent.

Our Feeling: We call it suffering and ask, "Why me?" A good parent is not moved by a child's tears when the environment is best for the child.

God's Arrangement: Everything is under the Father's hand. We can refuse the lie that it is mere pain and receive it as a blessing for growth into full sonship, knowing the Spirit has prayed for it ().
