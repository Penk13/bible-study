Romans · Message 28

Transformation in Receiving the Believers (1)

The Body revealed in Romans 12 must be lived out according to Romans 14. We receive believers according to God's receiving, not doctrinal concepts, and in the light of the judgment seat, judging ourselves rather than others.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • The Consummation: Creation, redemption, justification, sanctification, glorification, and selection all aim at one goal: the church life.
  • Transformation Needed: In our natural disposition we cannot even agree with ourselves; receiving others requires transformation.
  • God's Receiving: Whomever the Father has received, we must receive, however weak or peculiar ().
  • The Lord's: Whether we live or die, we are the Lord's; that matters, not diet, days, or doctrine ().
  • Judgment Seat: We will all give account of our life and work, so we judge ourselves, not our brother ().

Romans 12 Lived Out by Romans 14

Neglecting how we receive the saints is like caring for every part of the body except the one thing that can kill it. Many speak of the Body from Romans 12 yet cut it into pieces with the knife of doctrine. Paul gives all of chapter 14 and part of 15 to this warning. He knew the right view on food and days, yet he took no side; he charged both the strong and the weak not to despise or judge, but to receive ().

Divisive

By Doctrinal Concepts

Modes of baptism, head covering, holy days, foods: each has split Christians and turned churches into sects.

General

By God's Receiving

Children do not choose their siblings; the parents do. The only ground of receiving is that God has received him.

Food and Days

The worst concepts were those of religious Jewish believers: certain foods clean and others unclean, certain days holy and others common. Even Peter needed the vision three times before he heard, "What God has cleansed you must not call common" (). Such people think they are fighting for God's truth, but they frustrate God's building. The weak in faith have received less of God's infusing, yet they have a measure of faith and must be received. When doctrinal questions arise, help people turn from concepts to Christ as life.

In the Light of the Judgment Seat

The judgment seat of God in Romans 14 is the judgment seat of Christ (). It concerns only saved believers, not their salvation, which is eternal (). Yet a work of wood, hay, and stubble may be burned, and its builder suffer loss while being saved as through fire ().

The Bridge

Calvinism says once saved, no future problem; Arminianism says one may be lost again. The judgment seat spans the gap: eternally saved, yet accountable for reward or loss.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

What is the only proper ground for receiving a believer?Who chooses the siblings?

God's Receiving: If the Father has received him, we have no choice; we receive him too ().

What Matters: That he belongs to the Lord and lives to the Lord, not how he was baptized or which days he keeps.

How do sound doctrines become a knife against the Body?Fighting for the truth?

The Deviation: When a doctrine or practice becomes a requirement for fellowship, the church becomes a particular sect, and the Body is cut apart.

Paul's Example: He knew the truth about food and days, yet refused to pass judgment on reasonings, leaving each fully persuaded in his own mind.

How does the judgment seat differ from the eternal judgment?Saved, yet accountable.

Not Perdition: Believers cannot perish (). The white throne is for unbelievers.

Accountability: At Christ's return our life and work are reviewed, and we receive a reward or suffer loss ().

What does it mean today to receive others in the light of the judgment seat?Judge yourself.

Self-Judgment: It is easy to condemn others on one point while overlooking our own failures elsewhere. We will give account of ourselves ().

Attitude: We still exercise discernment, but we judge ourselves first and leave others to the Lord.
