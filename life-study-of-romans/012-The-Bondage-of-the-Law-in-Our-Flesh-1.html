Romans · Message 12

The Bondage of the Law in Our Flesh (1)

"You are not under law but under grace" (6:14), yet God never abolished the law. Romans 7:1–6 explains how: the first husband is our old man, who has died with Christ. Discharged from his law, we, the regenerated wife, are married to the risen Christ, bearing fruit to God and serving in newness of spirit.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Why Romans 7: Paul declared we are not under law (). Chapter 7 explains how, without the law being annulled.
  • Wife, Then Husband: Created as a wife to God (), fallen man assumed the husband's place, independent of God. The law was given to that husband.
  • The Law Is for Breaking: Not given to be kept but to expose: knowledge of sin (; ), so that the offense might abound ().
  • The First Husband: Not the law or the flesh, but the old man, crucified with Christ ( = ). Believers have two statuses: the old "I" dead, the new "I" alive ().
  • A Funeral and a Wedding: Dead to the law that we might marry the risen Christ (), take Him as Head (), bear fruit to God, and serve in newness of spirit ().

Who Is the First Husband?

For decades Witness Lee could not settle whether the first husband was the law or the flesh. The answer comes from the whole Bible and from noticing that a believer has two statuses.

Old Status

The Old "I"

Crucified with Christ; dead to the law (). When we were in the flesh, we bore fruit to death ().

New Status

The New "I"

"Nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me" (KJV, ). Married to Him who was raised from the dead.

The Purpose of the Law

We think the law was given to be kept. Scripture says otherwise:

VerseWhat the Law Does
Gives the knowledge of sin; justifies no one
Where there is no law, there is no transgression: it exposes
Entered that the offense might abound, not be restricted
"You shall not covet" revealed coveting as sin

No one can love their neighbor as themselves. Trying to keep the law is going to Hagar and producing Ishmael (). We are children not of the bondwoman but of the free ().

Living as the Wife

At a wedding there are two persons but one head; the bride's head is covered. Christ is our Husband, Head, person, and life.

🍇

Bearing Fruit to God

As the old husband everything we produced was death. Now all we are and do is related to God: God Himself overflowing as our fruit ().

✨

Serving in Newness of Spirit

Our regenerated spirit, where the Lord dwells (), is a source of newness. In meetings, exercise the spirit, not the memory ().

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

How can we be "not under law" if God never abolished the law?Read Romans 7:2–3: who dies, the law or the husband?

The Husband Died: The law of a husband binds a wife only while he lives (). The law remains; the old man to whom it was given was crucified with Christ ().

Discharged: So the new man, the wife, is free from that law and free to be joined to Christ ().

Why does Witness Lee say the law was given "to be broken"?Look at Romans 3:20, 4:15, 5:20.

To Expose: The law gives the knowledge of sin () and entered that the offense might abound (). Without it we would excuse our sins with pleasant names.

Not to Restrain: Fallen man cannot keep it. Its purpose is to show the "husband" what he really is, so that he will give up and turn to Christ.

How can we be both dead and alive?Notice "nevertheless," "yet," and "but" in Galatians 2:20.

Two Statuses: As the old man we are dead (); as the new man we live. "I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me" (KJV, ).

Die to Live, Die to Marry: In Galatians we die to live; in Romans 7:4 we die to marry. Both are possible only because a believer has an old and a new status.

What does it look like to "serve in newness of spirit" rather than "oldness of letter"?Think of how you speak or pray in a meeting.

Oldness: Serving from memory, regulations, and dead knowledge produces something stale, no matter how correct.

Newness: Our regenerated spirit, where the Lord is (), is a fountain of newness. Exercising it, rather than memory, brings something fresh and living ().
