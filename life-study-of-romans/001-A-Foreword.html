Life-Study / Romans 001

Romans · Message 1

A Foreword

The Bible is a pure and holy romance of a universal couple—the Triune God as Husband and His redeemed people as Bride. Romans defines how the individual Christ in the Gospels becomes the corporate Christ in church life through salvation, life, and building.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • The Holy Romance: The Bible is not primarily a textbook of ethics or doctrines, but a romance in the purest and most holy sense between God in Christ as the Bridegroom and His redeemed people as the Bride, consummating in the New Jerusalem ().
  • One Source, Nature, Life & Living: Typified by Adam and Eve (). Eve was built out of Adam's rib—she was of the same source, nature, and life. Likewise, the church is drawn entirely out of Christ's resurrected life to live together with Him ().
  • Position of Romans: The Gospels reveal the individual Christ; Acts reveals the spreading of Christ into hundreds of thousands; Romans provides the divine blueprint of how sinners are transformed into members of the corporate Christ (His Body).
  • The Eight Words / Sections: 1) Introduction (Gospel of God) → 2) Condemnation → 3) Justification → 4) Sanctification → 5) Glorification → 6) Selection → 7) Transformation → 8) Conclusion (Local Churches).
  • The Three Major Structures: Salvation (ch. 1:1–5:11; 9–11) is for Life (ch. 5:12–8:39), and Life is for Building (ch. 12–16)—the corporate expression of the Body in practical local church life!

The Secret of the Universe: A Holy Romance

Religion teaches man to fear, bow down, and worship an outward, distant Almighty. But the deep, intrinsic thought of the divine Word reveals that God’s deepest desire is to enter into a loving marriage with His chosen people. He does not want cold, ritualistic worshippers; He wants an affectionate counterpart who loves Him, takes Him as her life, and shares His living (; ; ).

The Couple: Love

Husband & Wife

Christ and the church as a couple are a matter of mutual affection and love. The wife takes her husband not only as her companion, but as her person, mind, and living ().

The Corporate Man: Life

Head & Body

Christ and the church as one universal man are a matter of organic life and function. Christ is the Head (), and the church is His Body, the fullness of Him who fills all in all ().

The Position of Romans in the Progression of the Bible

Where does Romans fit within the entire scope of the New Testament? Notice the clear divine progression from the individual Savior to the corporate Body:

👤

1. The Gospels

The Individual Christ: Over 60 divine titles reveal Christ in the flesh, walking on earth, dying for our redemption. He was among the disciples, but not yet inside them.

👥

2. The Acts

The Spreading Christ: The resurrection and ascension release His life to propagate into thousands of believers—Peter, John, Stephen, Saul of Tarsus—forming the corporate Christ.

🏛️

3. The Epistle to Romans

The Divine Blueprint: Unfolds the complete spiritual mechanics of how condemned sinners are justified, sanctified, and built together as living members of the Body in local church life!

The Eight Words Denoting the Eight Sections of Romans

Witness Lee highlights eight vital words that outline the master architecture of Romans. These eight sections guide the believer from condemnation in Adam to the glorious church life:

Section Passage Core Spiritual Content & Action
1. Introduction Rom. 1:1–17 The Gospel of God: Concerning His Son, promised in the Scriptures, revealed from faith to faith.
2. Condemnation Rom. 1:18—3:20 The Need of Salvation: The universal ruin of mankind under sin; total human hopelessness and helplessness.
3. Justification Rom. 3:21—5:11 The Accomplishment of Salvation: Propitiation, redemption, and reconciliation resulting in the full enjoyment of God ().
4. Sanctification Rom. 5:12—8:13 The Life-Process in Salvation: Christ within our spirit () saturating and "teaifying" our being with His divine nature!
5. Glorification Rom. 8:14–39 The Purpose of Salvation: Producing the many brothers to the Firstborn Son (); redemption of the body.
6. Selection Rom. 9:1—11:36 The Economy of Salvation: God's sovereign wisdom, calling, predestination, and eternal purpose.
7. Transformation Rom. 12:1—15:13 The Life-Practice in Salvation: Presenting our bodies, renewing our mind (), and practicing harmonious Body life.
8. Conclusion Rom. 15:14—16:27 The Ultimate Consummation: The local churches in actual practice—not a theoretical church, but churches in homes and cities ()!

The Analogy of Sanctification: Plain Water & Heavenly Tea

Many Christians mistake sanctification for mere outward behavioral improvement or moral self-discipline. Witness Lee provides this vivid illustration:

🥛

Plain Water (Our Natural Being)

In ourselves we are plain, dirty water. Even if cleansed by justification, plain water has no color, no tea aroma, and no divine flavor.

🍵

The Teaified Cup (Christ Saturated)

Christ is the heavenly tea dropped into our spirit (). As He steeps and diffuses into our mind, emotion, and will, our entire being becomes "teaified"—permeated with Christ's aroma, flavor, and essence!

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why does Witness Lee describe the Bible as a "holy romance of a universal couple," and how does this contrast with a religion of outward rituals and ethical duty?Recall the type of Adam and Eve (; ) and God's cry to Israel in the prophets (; ).

Core Biblical Truth: Religious man approaches God merely as the supreme Creator to be worshipped with fear, ceremonial offerings, and moral laws. But God revealed Himself throughout the Old and New Testaments as a Husband seeking a wife (; ; ). He was weary of empty temple rituals when Israel's heart did not love Him as her Husband.

The Typology of the Couple: Eve was not created independently; she was built out of Adam's rib taken from his opened side. Thus, they shared one source, one nature, one life, and one living. The church is drawn completely out of Christ's side—built of His resurrection life—to be His loving counterpart.

Spiritual Application: Our Christian life is not a cold performance of religious chores. God desires our affection, our kisses of love (), and our heart taking Him as our very person and living every day.

How does the progression of the seeker in the Song of Songs (from horses to a lily, a pillar of smoke, a palanquin, and a city) depict our transformation in Christ?Contrast the self-energy of Pharaoh's chariots () with the flexible, surrendered pillar of smoke () and the New Jerusalem ().

1. Company of Horses (): Represents the natural seeker—energetic, strong-willed, capable, but serving worldly Pharaoh with natural strength.

2. A Lily among Thorns (): Softened by divine love, living by faith without natural striving, blooming in pure beauty for Christ alone.

3. A Pillar of Smoke (): Erect and steadfast in heavenly standing, yet pliable and flexible without a rigid natural will—perfumed with the death (myrrh) and resurrection (frankincense) of Christ.

4. A Palanquin & A City (; ): Bearing the Person of Christ within her, becoming a garden that satisfies Him, and finally a fortified heavenly city—the New Jerusalem () where Christ is the sole Person and King.

Explain the "tea" illustration for sanctification. Why is sanctification fundamentally different from moral self-improvement or psychological self-control?Consider what happens when tea is put into plain water (), and reflect on your own daily dealings with others.

The Illustration: Plain water has no color, no fragrance, and no tea taste. Trying to "improve" the water by stirring it or cleaning the cup does not produce tea. But when tea leaves are placed in the water, the essence, color, and flavor of the tea saturate the water until the water is completely "teaified."

The Difference in Reality: Moral self-improvement is man's natural attempt to polish his flesh or restrain his bad temper. In contrast, biblical sanctification is Christ Himself as the heavenly tea being added into our spirit () and diffusing into our thoughts, loves, and decisions. It is not our natural behavior getting better, but Christ's divine element being wrought into our constitution!

How do the three major structures of Romans—Salvation, Life, and Building—protect us from stopping short in our Christian walk?Reflect on the connection between being justified (), enjoying the Spirit of life (), and functioning in the local church (; ).

The Danger of Stopping Short: Many believers stop at Salvation, thinking Christianity is merely being saved from hell and getting their sins forgiven. Other believers advance to Life, seeking personal holiness and inward peace, but remain spiritual individualists.

The Divine Goal: In Romans, salvation is for life, and life is for building. We are saved and regenerated so that the divine life may transform us to be built together with our fellow believers into the one Body of Christ (), which finds its practical, earthly expression in the local churches (). Without the church life, the goal of Romans is never reached.
