Romans · Message 31

A Closing Word

From creation in chapter one to the churches in chapter sixteen, God progresses until He reaches His peak in the local churches. Our station is there, with Christ as our unique concept and a watchful eye on division.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • God Progressively Revealed: Twelve situations, from God seen in creation to God expressed in the church life.
  • The Peak: The local churches are the consummation of God's work; Romans ends there with a melody of praise ().
  • Doctrine Divides, Life Unites: No Romans 12 without Romans 14; Christ must be our unique concept ().
  • The Last Station: Justification, sanctification, and Body talk are not the end; the Body is realized in the local churches.
  • Watch for Division: Turn away from those who divide with smooth, flattering words ().

The God in Romans

Romans unveils God step by step. He who is seen through His creation () becomes the God in our spirit (), then God in a corporate Body, and finally God expressed in the local churches.

The Consummation of God's Work

Creation took only a word, but producing the churches required a long process. With the churches God reaches His summit, so Romans ends at chapter sixteen with a melody, not a call to look away to rapture and heaven. Paul's gospel, unlike Mark or Luke, includes the churches: it has sixteen chapters, not eight or twelve. Romans is an abstract of the whole Bible, which runs from creation to the New Jerusalem (), the totality of the local churches.

Christ Our Unique Concept, and a Warning

Jewish believers brought religious tenets and Gentiles brought philosophy, so Paul told both to drop them and be likeminded according to Christ. Even sound, scriptural doctrines have cut Christianity into pieces; Christ is one. Paul's first commendation was of Phoebe in a particular church, for the church is practical, not a theory (). Yet even here the enemy works.

One Test

However smooth the words, ask: does this cause division contrary to the apostle's teaching? Such men serve their own appetites, not Christ (). Be simple, but alert.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why does Romans end with a melody instead of a look to the future?The peak is reached.

God's Satisfaction: With the local churches God's work has reached its summit; nothing higher remains.

Our Need: Not more, but to be established in what we have ().

Why are even sound doctrines divisive?Many doctrines, one Christ.

History: Every doctrine, right or wrong, has produced a sect when made a standard for fellowship.

The Way: Pray to be rescued from doctrinal concepts and take Christ alone as our concept ().

Why is Romans 12 not the final station?Where is the Body?

Empty Term: Without receiving the believers (ch. 14) and the local churches (ch. 16), "the Body" stays a theory.

Reality: The universal Body is expressed practically in localities as churches.

How can I stay simple yet not be deceived?Smooth and flattering speech.

The Test: Measure every talk by whether it divides and whether it contradicts the apostle's teaching.

The Response: Reject such talk and turn away from those who promote it ().
