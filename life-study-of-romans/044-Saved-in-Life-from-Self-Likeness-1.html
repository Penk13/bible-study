Romans · Message 44

Saved in Life from Self-Likeness (1)

Whatever we express naturally, good or bad, is the self. God saves us from self-likeness by conforming us to the image of His Son, a gradual, organic process of sanctification and transformation that issues in glorification.

Key verses

Rapid recall

  • Fact and Experience: Christ's death dealt with everything objectively; by having Him as life we are identified with it subjectively ().
  • Self-Likeness: The appearance of the self; natural love and natural hatred share the same essence.
  • Prototype and Copies: The one Son was designated (); the many sons are being conformed to His image ().
  • Glory by Growth: Glorification is the consummation of a process, from glory to glory ().
  • All Things Together: God works everything, our spouse and environment included, for this goal ().

The Expression of the Self

Right after calling Peter "Satan," the Lord spoke of denying the self (). As Christ embodies and expresses God, the self embodies and expresses Satan. We were not only born with a self; we were born a self. The cross has sentenced it; we must execute that judgment in experience.

Conformed to the Son's Image

Romans 8 speaks of children who cry "Abba," sons led by the Spirit (), and mature heirs. We are conformed not to the image of children or heirs, but of the Son. Through incarnation, death, and resurrection the only begotten became the Firstborn among many brothers.

🧩

The Prototype

The one Son designated in resurrection ().

👥

Mass Production

The many sons conformed to His image ().

Glorification Is a Process

The central thought of Romans is not justification but God making sinners sons to form the Body. In 8:30 justification includes propitiation, redemption, and reconciliation, while glorification includes sanctification and transformation (). Glory appears at His coming (), but like a seed it develops by stages: sprout, growth, blossom.

💧

Plain Water

Natural, whether clean or dirty. God's aim is not to make us better water.

🍵

Tea

Christ, the heavenly tea, permeates us until our taste and appearance are changed: an organic, metabolic change.

God Does the Work

He foreknew, predestinated, called, justified, and glorified; even glorification is in the past tense, for with God there is no time element. When we do not know how to pray for the saints, the Spirit intercedes with groanings for their conformation ().

Everything Is His Best

Our spouse, our job or lack of one, our sufferings: the Father is never mistaken. All things work together for good (), and what He has done in us cannot be undone.

Reflect

Think each question through first, then open it to compare.

Why is natural love no better than natural hatred in God's eyes?Same source.

Same Essence: Both come out of the self, and the self is the expression of Satan ().

True Expression: Only what comes out of Christ's life expresses Christ.

If Christ's death has dealt with the self, why do I still need saving?Objective vs. subjective.

The Fact: On the cross the self was judged once for all.

The Experience: Having Christ as life identifies us with His death, executing that judgment day by day ().

How is glorification both sudden and gradual?The seed and the blossom.

Appearing: The full glory is manifested when the Lord comes ().

Growing: Yet it is the consummation of transformation from glory to glory ().

How should I view the people and situations I dislike?Working together.

God's Best: Every environment is His arrangement to conform me to the Son.

Rest and Praise: The work is His; I can rest and thank Him ().
